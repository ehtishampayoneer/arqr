How Canadian Decor Stores Sell After Closing with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Toronto. She sells wall mirrors, lamps, vases and wall art from one shop and a website. Her doors close at 5pm. Her website stays open all night, and that is when most of her shoppers browse. They sit on the sofa after dinner, phone in hand, looking at decor for the living room. They like a mirror, they check the price, and then they stop. They cannot tell if it will suit their wall, so they close the tab and say they will think about it.

Most of them never come back. The sale was lost at 9pm, hours after the shop closed, by a question nobody was there to answer. Here is how a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page answers it for them, while you sleep.

Your shop closes. Your website does not.

Decor shopping happens in the evening. People look at their bare walls after work, they browse while the kids are in bed, they talk it over with their partner on the sofa. That is when the buying decision happens, and it happens on a phone, not in your shop. A product page with flat photos and a list of dimensions asks the shopper to guess. A mirror or a lamp is a big part of the room. Shoppers do not guess on a big part of the room. They wait, and waiting usually means never.

Think about what your shop does in the daytime that your website cannot do at night. A customer in the shop can hold the lamp up, stand back from the mirror, see it against her sofa, and ask you a question. At 9pm on a phone, none of that happens. The AR (Augmented Reality) view brings the shop to her living room. She taps the link, points her phone at her wall, and the piece appears at its true size. She walks around it. She checks it against the sofa and the side table. She sees the colours in her own light. The size question is answered in seconds, with nobody on the phone and nobody in the shop.

What an evening shopper needs to see

Three things decide a decor sale after closing. The size, the colour in real light, and the feel of the piece in the room. The AR view answers the first two on the spot. The size is exact, because our designing department builds each view from the real listed dimensions of the piece. The colour shows in the light of the shopper's own room, not a studio photo. The piece is the piece itself, placed at full size on her wall or her table. That is enough for most shoppers to stop guessing and start buying.

It also answers the question shoppers are too polite to ask in the shop: will this look wrong in my room? A photo cannot answer that. A piece at true size in their own room can.

Where the links go

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where the size doubt lives. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters, even at midnight.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. A customer who likes a mirror in the shop can check it at home that evening and order it from her sofa.
  • In your evening posts and emails. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your evening shoppers browse. A short evening post with the link in it reaches shoppers at the hour they actually buy.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and evening shoppers hesitate most of all, because there is nobody to ask.
