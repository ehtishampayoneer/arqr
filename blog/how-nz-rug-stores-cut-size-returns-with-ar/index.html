How New Zealand Rug Stores Cut Size Returns with True-to-Size AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Christchurch. She sells wool rugs and runners online and from one shop. Every month a few rugs come back. Not because of faults. Because of size. The rug that looked right on the screen swallows the living room. The runner that looked generous barely reaches the hallway table. Each return costs her freight both ways, a rug out of stock while it travels, and a customer who may never order again.

This is the plain version of how a New Zealand rug store cuts those size returns with AR (Augmented Reality), and keeps more of the sales it already won.

Why size returns hurt a rug store more than most shops

A rug is bulky, heavy, and awkward to ship. A returned pair of socks fits in a satchel. A returned rug needs a courier, careful rolling, and a restock check when it arrives. It comes back creased, sometimes scuffed, and it goes back up at a discount or not at all. The store pays the freight twice and keeps nothing.

The deeper cost is the lost customer. A shopper who waited a week for a rug, unrolled it, and saw it was wrong does not just want a refund. She now doubts every rug on the site. The next time she shops, she goes to a store she can walk into. True-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) answers the doubt before the order, while the shopper is still excited about the rug.

What the shopper sees

Each rug gets a link and a QR code that opens a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view in the phone browser. No app to install. The shopper points their phone at their own floor and the rug appears there at its real size, in their own light, beside their own sofa and coffee table.

That check is the whole sale for rugs. A shopper can see whether the front legs of the sofa sit on the rug or off it. She can see whether the runner leaves enough bare floor on each side. She can try the next size up without ordering two rugs and sending one back. When the answer is yes, she buys with confidence. When the answer is no, she picks a different size, and the store keeps a sale instead of processing a return.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and for rugs, hesitation is what turns into a wrong-size order.

What changes for the store

  • Fewer size returns. The buyer who saw the rug at its true size on their own floor is far less likely to be surprised on delivery day.
  • Fewer will it fit messages. Staff stop answering the same sizing questions by email, because the view answered them on the page.
  • Bigger rugs sell with confidence. Large rugs carry the biggest freight and the biggest return risk. A true-to-size view is what lets a shopper commit to the 3 by 4 instead of playing safe with the small one.
  • Distant shoppers buy like locals. A customer in Dunedin or Invercargill gets the same size confidence as a customer standing in the shop.
  • Shop visitors who come back decided. A QR code on the shop tag lets a visitor check the rug at home in the evening and order from the sofa.
