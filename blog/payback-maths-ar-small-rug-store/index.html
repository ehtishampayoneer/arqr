The Payback Maths: How a Small Rug Store Covers the Cost of AR (Augmented Reality) in Weeks

Picture a rug store owner in Denver. She sells handmade rugs online. Average order, about $600. Every month she ships around 40 rugs and gets 8 back. Not because of defects. Because of size. The runner that looked right on the screen swallows the hallway. The rug that looked small online dwarfs the coffee table.

So she asks the plain question. AR (Augmented Reality) shows each rug at its true size in the buyer's own room, before they order. Fine. But what does it cost her, and what does it pay back? This is the maths for one example store. Example numbers, not a promise. Run your own at the end.

Where the money comes back: returns you never ship

A returned rug costs more than the refund. It ships twice. It comes back rolled, creased, sometimes scuffed. It goes back up used and sells at a discount. Picture the Denver store: 8 size returns a month. Two-way shipping on a rug, call it $80 a round trip. That is $640 a month burned on rugs going out and coming back, before any discount loss.

Now picture the same store after buyers could see the rug at its true size first. Say it trims those 8 returns to 6. Two rugs a month not coming back. That is $160 a month saved on shipping alone. The $597 first-year cost is behind her in under four months. Run it with your own return count and your own shipping cost. The numbers change. The shape does not.

Where the money comes back: buyers who were going to leave

Returns are only half the maths. Size doubt also kills sales that never show up as returns. The shopper who likes a 5 by 8, cannot tell if it will fit her room, and closes the tab. Nothing to refund. Nothing to ship. The sale just never happened.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Take the example store again. If AR turns just two extra browsers a month into buyers, at $600 a rug, that is $1,200 in sales against a $597 first-year cost. You do not need anywhere near 94% to win the maths. A handful of saved sales covers it.

What makes the maths work, or fail

  • Bestsellers first. Twenty proven rugs covering most of your revenue do more than the whole catalogue on day one.
  • Put the view next to the price. That is where sizing doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • No app for the shopper. If the customer has to install an app, most will not, and your views never pay back. Each rug should open in the phone browser from a link or a QR code. Try our live rug demo on your own phone.
  • True-to-size proof. A rug that is even a few inches off teaches the buyer the tool cannot be trusted. Ask every provider how they check each view against the real listed dimensions.
