How UK Home Decor Stores End Wrong-Size Returns with True-to-Size AR

Picture a home decor store owner in Bath. She sells table lamps, wall mirrors, vases, and framed prints. The shop does steady weekend trade, and the website takes orders from all over the country. Every week a few pieces come back, and the reason is almost always the same. It looked bigger online.

Decor returns are rarely about taste. The shopper loved the lamp. The lamp was simply too tall for the side table. The mirror was right, the wall was wrong. A framed print that looked perfect in the listing photo disappears against a big fireplace. Nobody measured anything. They guessed, and the guess was off.

Why decor pieces come back

Photos flatten scale. A vase photographed alone on a white background could be 20 centimetres or 40, and the customer has no way to tell. A round mirror looks the same at every size in a square listing photo. Dimensions are listed, of course, but almost nobody holds a tape measure up to a wall before ordering. They look at the photo, picture it in the room, and trust the picture.

The picture is usually wrong. And a decor return costs more than most. A mirror needs careful packing both ways. A ceramic vase can arrive back in pieces. The store pays the shipping, the item comes back a little less new each time, and the margin on the sale is gone twice over.

What the shopper checks before they order

The true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view answers the one question the photo cannot. The customer taps a link on the product page, points the phone at their own wall or table, and the piece appears at its real size, live in their own room. They walk up to it. They check the mirror against the actual wall. They see the lamp on the actual side table next to the actual sofa. No tape measure, no guessing.

No app to download. It opens in the phone browser, in seconds. That matters, because most of this happens late in the evening, on the sofa, when the shopper is one tap away from either ordering or giving up.

Shopify reports that products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. That is Shopify's own data, not ours. For decor the mechanism is simple. A shopper who has seen the true size of a piece in their own room hesitates less and guesses less.

Where the store puts the links

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where the sizing doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button answers the question before the shopper leaves.
