How Canadian Furniture Stores Cut Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a furniture store owner in Ottawa. She sells sofas, armchairs, and coffee tables. Her delivery truck covers the whole region, and some orders ship out of province. Every month a few pieces come back. The reason is almost always the same. It did not fit the room.

A furniture return hurts more than most. A sofa that goes to Montreal and comes back has burned two long trips, a packing job, and a delivery slot she could have sold to a real customer. The piece comes back a little less new each time. The margin on the sale is gone twice over.

Why furniture comes back

Photos flatten scale. A three-seat sofa photographed in a big studio could be two metres or two and a half, and the customer has no way to tell. The dimensions are listed, of course. Almost nobody holds a tape measure up to their living room wall before ordering. They look at the photo, picture it in the room, and trust the picture.

The picture is usually wrong. The sectional is too deep for the room. The armchair blocks the doorway. The coffee table looked right in the listing and wrong next to the real sofa. Nobody is angry. They just guessed, and the guess was off.

What the shopper checks before they order

The true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view answers the one question the photo cannot. The customer taps a link on the product page, points the phone at their own floor, and the piece appears at its real size, live in their own room. They walk around it. They check the sofa against the actual wall. They see if the armchair blocks the doorway before they buy it.

No app to download. It opens in the phone browser, in seconds. That matters, because most of this happens late in the evening, on the sofa, when the shopper is one tap away from either ordering or giving up.

Shopify reports that products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. That is Shopify's own data, not ours. For furniture the mechanism is simple. A shopper who has seen the true size of a sofa in their own room hesitates less and guesses less.

Where the store puts the links

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where the sizing doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button answers the question before the shopper leaves.
  • In Instagram and WhatsApp chats. Canadian shoppers ask questions in DMs before they buy. An AR link in the chat lets them check the piece on their own floor without a trip to the shop.
  • On the floor model tags in the store. A QR code on the tag lets browsing customers check the piece at home and come back decided. The sale survives the trip home.
