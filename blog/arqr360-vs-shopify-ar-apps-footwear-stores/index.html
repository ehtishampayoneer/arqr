ARQR360 vs Shopify AR Apps: What a Shoe Store Actually Gets from Each

Picture a shoe store owner in Chicago. She sells running shoes and sneakers from a shop and a Shopify website. A customer asks if he can see a shoe next to his own foot before ordering two sizes and returning one. She likes the idea. Then she finds the Shopify AR route has two doors, not one.

This is the honest version.

What the Shopify AR route actually is

There is the free door and the app door. Shopify themes can show 3D and AR on product pages at no extra cost. The viewer is built in. You upload a 3D file, GLB or USDZ, and the shopper gets a 3D view with an AR button. That part is free.

The app door is the Shopify app store. Dozens of apps add viewers, AI generation, and AR buttons: ARDisplay, Arty, Easy 3D Viewer, Voxelwave, and more. Each has its own plan and its own product caps.

The free door: your theme shows it, but you bring the files

The free route only works if you have 3D files. Shopify accepts GLB and USDZ up to 500 MB, auto-optimizes anything over 15 MB, and its partner docs aim for about 4 MB per file. Most shoe stores do not have files. They have photos.

Then there is the size question. Shopify's own guidance tells merchants to check each model's scale in AR before it goes live. If the shoe renders five percent off, your shopper learns nothing about size. The check is your job, file by file. A store with 60 styles is checking 60 files.

The app door: viewers from about $9 a month, generation from about $29

Viewer apps are the cheaper door. Easy 3D Viewer runs free for 3 products, then about $9 to $36 a month by product count. Arty starts around $19 a month. You still supply the 3D files.

AI-generation apps build the files from your photos. ARDisplay lists a free plan for 5 products, then $29 a month for 25 products with 20 generated files a month, $109 for 150 products. Voxelwave lists a free single file, then $49 a month for 10. App prices move, so check the listing before you budget.

The catch is the same as the free door. Auto-generated files still need your eye. One listing advertises an auto-size generator, which is worth testing yourself against a real shoe before you trust it across a catalogue. For footwear the bar is high: a few millimeters off is a size wrong.

Which store should pick which

The Shopify routes fit a store that already holds true-to-size 3D files, or has someone in-house who enjoys the file pipeline. If the files are in hand, the free viewer is genuinely free and worth using.

FAQ

Do I need 3D files for the Shopify AR apps?

For viewer apps, yes: you upload the GLB or USDZ files. Some apps generate the files from your photos instead, with monthly caps on the entry plans. ARQR360 needs neither from you: product photos and real dimensions are enough, and we produce the AR (Augmented Reality) views. The first view is free.

Can AR tell a shopper how a shoe fits?

Honestly, no. AR shows true size, not comfort, not feel, not arch support. What it does well is give the shopper a real size idea of the shoe against their own foot, which is what stops the two-size orders.
