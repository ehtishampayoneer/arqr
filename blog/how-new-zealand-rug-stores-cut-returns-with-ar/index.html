How New Zealand Rug Stores Cut Size Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Auckland. She sells wool rugs and handwoven runners from one shop and a website. Orders come in from Wellington, Christchurch, Dunedin, and small towns in between. Every month a few rugs come back. The reason is almost always the same. The size was wrong.

A rug return in New Zealand hurts more than most. A rug that goes to Dunedin and comes back has burned two long trips across the country, a packing job, and a delivery slot she could have sold to a real customer. Freight on a rolled rug is not cheap, and a returned rug comes back a little less new each time.

Why rugs come back

A photo cannot show true size. A rug shot in a studio looks the same at 160 by 230 as it does at 200 by 290, and the shopper has no way to tell from the picture. The measurements sit on the page, but almost nobody tapes out the floor before ordering. They look at the photo, imagine the rug under their coffee table, and trust the picture.

The picture is usually wrong. The rug is too big, so it swallows the room and creeps under the sofa. Or it is too small, so it floats in the middle of the floor and looks lost. Nobody is angry. They just guessed, and the guess was off.

What the shopper checks before they order

The true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view answers the one question the photo cannot. The customer taps a link on the product page, points the phone at the floor, and the rug appears at its real size, live in their own room. They check it against the coffee table, the sofa, and the doorway. They see whether the pattern sits right in the light they actually live in.

No app to download. It opens in the phone browser, in seconds. That matters, because most of this happens late in the evening, on the sofa, when the shopper is one tap away from either ordering or giving up.

Shopify reports that products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. That is Shopify's own data, not ours. For rugs the mechanism is simple. A shopper who has seen the exact rug on their own floor hesitates less and guesses less.

Where the store puts the links

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • In Instagram chats and email replies. Shoppers ask about sizing in DMs before they buy. An AR link in the chat lets them check the rug on their own floor without a trip to the shop.
  • On the swing tags and in the shop. A QR code on the tag lets browsing customers check another size at home and come back decided. The sale survives the trip home.
