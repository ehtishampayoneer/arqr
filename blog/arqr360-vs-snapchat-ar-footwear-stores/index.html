ARQR360 vs Snapchat AR: What a Shoe Store Actually Gets from Each

Picture a shoe store owner in Austin. He sells sneakers and boots from a small shop and a website that ships across Texas. His customers keep asking if they can try shoes on at home. He hears about two kinds of AR. One is Snapchat, where brands run those fun try-on lenses. The other is ARQR360, the done-for-you AR catalogue for stores like his. He wonders which one is actually meant for a store like his.

This is the honest version.

What Snapchat's AR actually is

Snap has been doing AR longer than almost anyone. Its Lens Studio is a free desktop app for building AR lenses on Snapchat. Back in 2020 Snap added a foot-tracking template powered by Wannaby's ML model, made exactly for sneaker try-on. Gucci used that tech for a shoppable sneaker lens that Snap and Wannaby say reached 18.9 million people. The quality of that try-on is genuinely good. Feet are the one thing phone AR tracks really well.

Snap also offers AR Shopping templates, free for brands that import their own AR-ready assets. The catch is distribution. All of it lives inside the Snapchat app. Your customer has to open Snapchat, find your lens, and play with it there. It is not on your product page.

One more piece of history worth knowing. In March 2023 Snap launched AR Enterprise Services, a Shopping Suite meant to bring its try-on tech to brands' own websites and apps. Six months later it shut the whole thing down, with the CEO saying generative AI had made it too hard to differentiate. So if you were waiting for Snap to sell you AR for your own store, that door closed. The lens route is the route that is left.

Price: free software vs one public flat rate

Lens Studio is free. That is real and worth saying. But the free software is not the whole bill. You need 3D assets of your shoes first. Snap's own template pages say brands must import AR-ready assets. If you do not have those, a 3D artist makes them, roughly a few hundred dollars per product, the same maths we worked through in our footwear pricing breakdown. Then you need someone who knows Lens Studio, or a creative partner, and if you want reach, the shoppable lens is an ad product, so you are paying for ads too.

ARQR360 is flat and public: $199 one-time setup and $19 a month, locked for life for the first 50 stores, with every AR (Augmented Reality) view built and hosted as part of it. No product cap. No per-view fee. Your first view is free.

Setup effort: build it yourself vs send photos

The Snap route is a project. You get your shoes made as 3D assets, someone builds the lens, and then you need customers to discover it inside Snapchat. For a big brand with a social team, that is a campaign. For a one-shop retailer, it is a second job.

The ARQR360 route is a photoshoot. You send product photos and your real listed sizes. About seven days later you get links and QR codes for each shoe. You paste the link next to the buy button. That is the whole integration.

True-to-size accuracy

Fair is fair. Snap's sneaker try-on, the Wannaby tech, is good at shoes on feet. That is the problem it was built for.

ARQR360 answers a different sizing question. A try-on lens answers "do these look good on me." A true-to-size AR view answers "am I ordering the right pair." For footwear that means the real shape and size in the customer's space, opened from your product page with no app. Different answers, both useful. Most shoe returns are not about looks. They are about the wrong pair in the box. We worked through that in our piece on how UK shoe stores cut size returns.

Who builds the 3D models

This is the question that decides everything. On the Snap route, you do. You supply the AR-ready 3D assets, or you hire someone to make them. On the ARQR360 route, nobody on your side does: you send photos, we build the AR (Augmented Reality) views from your product photos and your listed sizes.

For a store with a 3D team or a creative agency on retainer, the Snap route is a fun campaign. For a store where the owner takes the product photos on a phone, the done-for-you route is the only one that ships.

The honest verdict

Snap's AR is the most fun AR in the world, and the most reach you can get for it, inside one app. If you want a campaign that lives on Snapchat and reaches young shoppers, build a lens. If you want the customer on your own product page to stop guessing and buy, put the true-to-size view next to your buy button. One is a reach channel. The other is a conversion tool. They are not rivals. They just live in different places.
