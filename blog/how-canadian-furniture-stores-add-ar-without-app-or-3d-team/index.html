How Canadian Furniture Stores Add AR (Augmented Reality) Without an App or a 3D Team

Picture a furniture store owner in Mississauga. She sells sofas, dining sets, and bedroom furniture online and from one showroom. Most of her website visitors live nowhere near the shop. They browse the photos, wonder if the sofa will fit their living room, and leave. She has heard about AR for years. Every version she has seen needed an app, a developer, or a 3D team. She has none of those. So the idea stayed in the maybe pile.

This is the honest version.

Why the AR question feels harder than it is

AR sounds like a software project. Software projects mean developers, budgets, timelines, and meetings. A furniture store is none of those things. It is sales staff, a showroom, deliveries, and a website that has to pay for itself. The store owner is right to park anything that smells like a project.

But AR for a furniture store is not a software project. Not anymore. There is a done-for-you route where the store does almost nothing. The store sends photos. Everything after that happens on the service side. This is how it works, step by step, with nothing hidden.

Step one: you send photos of your bestsellers

It starts with what you already have. Photos of your bestselling pieces, from the angles your website already shows, plus the real listed dimensions of each piece. No special photography. No 3D scanning. No studio setup. If the piece is on your website, you already have what is needed.

Start with your bestsellers, not the whole catalogue. Twenty proven pieces cover most of a store's revenue. The rest can follow once you see what customers do with the first twenty.

Step two: our designing department builds the AR views

This is the part most stores imagine is their job. It is not. Our designing department takes your photos and dimensions and produces true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views of each piece. True to size means the sofa that is 210 cm wide appears 210 cm wide in the customer's room. A view that is even slightly off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted, so every view is checked against the real dimensions before it ships.

Your first AR view is free. Put it on your own phone, stand next to the real piece, and check the size yourself. If it does not convince you, you have spent nothing.

Step three: you get links and QR codes that just work

Each finished piece comes back to you as a link and a QR code. The link goes on the product page, next to the price, where the buying decision happens. The QR code goes on the price tag in the showroom, in your social posts, on packaging, on printed flyers. Everywhere the customer meets the product, the same AR view opens.

No app. Not for you, not for your customer. The AR view opens in the phone browser in seconds. If your customer had to install an app to see your sofa, most of them never would. People do not install apps to look at furniture. Browser AR gets used. App AR gets skipped. Try the live furniture demo on your own phone and see how fast it opens.

The follow-ups are part of the service, not an extra

Catalogues change. New ranges arrive, colours sell out, fabrics rotate. A done-for-you service that disappears after launch is a bill with no future. With ARQR360 the follow-up is built in. When a new piece arrives, you send its photo. Our designing department builds its AR view and it joins your catalogue. The service keeps running while you keep selling.

Custom packages are the same story. If you sell modular sectionals, adjustable pieces, or fabric ranges, the AR views cover the combinations your customers actually ask about, not just the one on the showroom floor. The floor shows one fabric of the sofa. The AR catalogue shows the rest.

Where the links and QR codes go

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button turns a sizing question into a two-minute answer.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. Customers standing in front of one fabric check the other five at home and come back decided.
  • In your chats and emails. Customers ask about size by message as much as they read the website. An AR link answers the question with the thing itself.
