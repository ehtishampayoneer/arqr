What European Decor Stores Look for in an AR Provider

Picture a decor store owner in Lyon. She sells wall art, mirrors, vases and lamps from one shop and a website that ships across Europe. She is ready to put AR (Augmented Reality) on her product pages, and three vendors have sent her quotes. The quotes do not look like they describe the same product. Here is the checklist that cuts through them.

It comes down to five checks. Who produces the views. Whether the shopper needs an app. How true size is proven. What the real price is for one year. And whether the provider stays after launch. Ask all five in writing before you sign anything.

1. Who produces the views, and from what

Start here, because everything else follows. Ask the vendor a plain question: who makes the AR views of my pieces, and what do they start from?

On some routes, you supply the files yourself. The vendor sells a viewer, you commission the files from artists, and you manage the whole pipeline. For a 200 piece catalogue, that bill is the project, not the software.

On the done-for-you route, you send photos and your real listed dimensions, and the vendor produces the true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views. ARQR360 works this way. Our designing department produces the views for you, from your photos, with new season stock handled the same way. When you compare quotes, compare the full job: views plus viewer plus hosting, not the monthly fee alone.

2. Does the shopper need an app

Ask before you sign: does the customer need to download an app to open the view? If the answer is yes, most of them will not use it. People do not install apps to check a mirror. A view that opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code, gets used. Test that on your own phone with our live decor demo.

3. How is true size proven

For decor, size is the whole question. A wall art piece that is even a little off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted. Ask the vendor how they check every view against the real listed dimensions, and what happens when a piece is off. A serious answer names a process. A vague answer is the answer.

Ask too what the shopper actually sees. The honest promise for decor is size and look: the piece at its true size, on the shoppers own wall or shelf, so they can compare it with the room they already have. AR cannot tell a shopper how a piece will feel in the hand, and any vendor who promises that is overselling. What it can do, and do well, is end the size guessing that stops so many orders.

4. What does one year actually cost

Get the quote in writing for your full catalogue, for one year, with everything included. The monthly fee is usually the smallest line. The rest is per-piece production fees, per-view charges that grow with your traffic, setup fees, and the cost of new season stock. A quote that will not put that number in writing is a quote you cannot compare.

For reference, ARQR360 is flat and public: $249 one-time setup and $29 a month, with all AR (Augmented Reality) view production included. No product cap, no per-view fees. 5. Does the provider stay after launch

AR is not a one off purchase. New stock arrives, bestsellers change, and the catalogue keeps moving. Ask the vendor who handles the next batch of pieces, how fast they go live, and who you call when a view stops working. A provider who disappears after the setup fee leaves you with a viewer and no views.

ARQR360 follows up after launch. New season pieces get the same treatment as the first batch, and the team checks the views are working on the pages where shoppers actually see them. That follow up is part of the service, not an extra.

Where the view goes on the page

A view that shoppers cannot find does nothing. Put it where the buying decision happens.

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your shoppers browse.

Start with your bestsellers. Twenty proven pieces covering most of your revenue do more than the whole catalogue on day one. The pieces that bring the most browsers are the pieces that most need the view.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less.
