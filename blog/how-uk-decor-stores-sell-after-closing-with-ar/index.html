How UK Decor Stores Sell After Closing with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Leeds. She sells lamps, mirrors, vases and wall art from one shop and a website. The shop closes at 5pm. Her website does not close, and her shoppers do most of their browsing after dinner, on the sofa, on their phones. That is when the doubt arrives. Will the lamp suit the side table. Will the mirror suit the wall. Will the vase look right on the console. By morning, the feeling has passed, and the tab is closed.

This is the plain version of how a decor store sells after closing, without staying open late.

The evening problem

Evening browsing is relaxed browsing, and relaxed browsing is where decor gets bought. The shopper is sitting in the very room the piece would live in. They can look up from the phone and see the side table, the wall, the console. What they cannot do from a photo is judge size. A lamp that looks fine on a white background can look lost on a real side table. A mirror that looks neat on screen can swallow a hallway wall. So they bookmark it, or they leave it, and they mean to come back. Most of them do not.

The shop loses nothing it can see. No angry message, no abandoned basket email. Just a quiet sale that never happened, because the shopper could not answer one question at 9pm: will this suit my room, at this size.

What the shopper sees after hours

AR (Augmented Reality) answers that question in the room itself. Each piece gets a link and a QR code that opens a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view in the phone browser. No app to install. The shopper points their phone at their own side table and the lamp appears there at its real size, in their own light, beside their own sofa.

That check is worth more at night than any description. The shopper sees the lamp against their own wall colour, the mirror above their own console, the vase beside their own sofa. Spatial fit is the honest promise for decor: does this piece suit this spot, at this size, in this room. When the answer is yes, the shopper can buy there and then, while the feeling is warm. When the answer is no, they move on without ordering the wrong size, which saves the store a return.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and evening shoppers who hesitate until morning usually do not come back.

What changes for the store

  • Sales after closing. The product page keeps working when the shop is shut, because the size question gets answered on the page itself.
  • Fewer will it suit messages. Staff stop answering the same sizing questions the next morning, because the view answered them the night before.
  • Fewer size returns. The buyer who saw the mirror at its true size on their own wall is far less likely to be surprised on delivery day.
  • Shop visitors who come back decided. A QR code on the shop tag lets a daytime visitor check the piece at home in the evening and order from the sofa.
