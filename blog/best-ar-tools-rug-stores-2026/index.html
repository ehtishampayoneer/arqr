The Best AR Tools for Rug Stores in 2026

Picture a rug store owner in Denver. She sells handmade and machine-made rugs, from small runners to big 12x15 statement pieces. Her website gets steady traffic. Visitors open a rug page, zoom into the pattern, read the size guide, then close the tab. The rug sits unsold.

The problem is not the rug. It is the one question a flat photo cannot answer. Will this rug actually work in my room. A rug two inches too narrow looks wrong the moment it unrolls. A pattern that is perfect online can fight with the sofa in real life. Customers know this, so they hesitate.

AR answers the size question before the order ships. The customer points their phone at their own floor and sees the rug in place, at its true size, next to their own furniture. Here are the real options a rug store can buy in 2026, written for a store owner, not a developer.

Option 1: photo-based room visualizers

The best-known example in rugs is Roomvo, a Canadian room-visualization platform used by flooring and rug retailers. Your catalogue plugs into their system, and your website gets an embedded visualizer. The customer picks a rug, uploads a photo of their own room or chooses a stock room scene, and sees the rug layered into that photo. They can swap rugs, compare side by side, adjust the size, and share the finished image.

It is photo visualization, not 3D. Nobody builds a 3D model of anything. The visualizer composites your real product photos into the customer's room photo. It runs in the browser on your own website, with your branding on it. No app to download.

Good when: you want customers to see your actual rug photo in their actual room, including the exact pattern and color they would buy. For rugs, a photo composite shows the real thing, which matters. Bad when: you need the rug placed on the floor of a live camera view. A room photo is one fixed angle. Also check the pricing and catalogue terms in writing before you compare, since it is a platform subscription built for retailers, not a per-item service.

Option 2: Shopify AR viewer apps

If you sell on Shopify, AR viewer apps on the app store add a 3D viewer to your product pages for a small monthly subscription. Public listings put pricing around free to roughly $36 a month, depending on how many products you publish.

The catch is the same one every viewer app has. The apps are viewers only. You supply every 3D model yourself, usually commissioned from 3D artists at roughly $100 to $500 per product. A rug store with 150 designs needs 150 models for full coverage. The subscription is small. The modelling bill is the project. We worked the same maths in our pricing breakdown for rug stores.

Good when: you already have 3D models of your rugs, or a designer on staff who makes them. Bad when: you do not, which is almost every small rug store.

Option 3: 3D and AR creation services

Some companies build the 3D models and host the AR for you. The usual shape: you pay per model for creation, roughly a couple hundred dollars a model depending on complexity, plus a SaaS fee for hosting and distribution that scales with your catalogue size. The pricing is honest about what it costs. It costs what enterprise budgets are built for.

Good when: you run a brand with hundreds of designs and the budget for per-model creation plus platform fees. Bad when: you run a small store, where the creation bill is the whole project before anything earns a dollar.

Good when: you want AR live this month with no new hires. Bad when: you need deep custom work like a full product configurator. That is a platform job, not a service job.

What to check before you choose

  • True-to-size proof: for rugs, a size off by a few inches is the whole purchase. Ask every provider how they verify the rug's real dimensions against what the customer sees.
  • Photo or live AR: a visualizer shows your rug in a room photo. AR shows it on the customer's real floor through the phone camera. Both answer the size question. Only AR answers it from every angle.
  • Who makes the visuals: the biggest cost on most routes is model creation, not the software. Ask whether the work is included and what new-season stock costs.
  • Total cost, not the monthly fee: add the models, the setup, and the new range. Per-model fees quietly dwarf subscriptions on catalogues that change.

Where each option lands

If you want customers to see your actual rug photo in their room photo with your branding, a visualizer platform like Roomvo is the purpose-built tool.
