Why Furniture Stores Need AR Now, and How ARQR360 Fills the Gap

Every furniture store knows the same expensive problem. A shopper falls in love with a sofa online, orders it, and sends it back because it looked bigger in the photos. Size and fit returns are the most expensive returns in furniture retail. The delivery truck goes out, the truck comes back, and the store eats the cost both ways.

This is exactly the problem AR (Augmented Reality) was made to solve. A shopper points their phone at their own living room and sees your sofa there, at its true size, before they buy. The guesswork disappears. The returns drop. The confident buyers buy faster.

Why most stores still do not have it

If AR is this obvious, why does almost every independent furniture store still sell from flat photos? Because until now, getting AR meant one of three bad options.

Option one: enterprise platforms built for giant brands. Quoted pricing, onboarding projects, contracts shaped for companies with product teams. A store with sixty pieces and two people on the floor does not need that machinery and cannot afford it.

Option two: viewer apps that need you to supply your own 3D files. If you do not have a 3D team, and almost no independent store does, you are stuck before you start.

Option three: per-model services that build you a file and disappear. Nobody follows up, nobody updates anything, and the next ten pieces you add are your problem.

That gap between what AR can do and what a real store can actually buy is the whole reason ARQR360 exists.
