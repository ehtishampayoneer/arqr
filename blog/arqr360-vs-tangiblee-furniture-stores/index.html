ARQR360 vs Tangiblee: What a Furniture Store Actually Gets from Each

Picture a furniture store owner in Manchester. She runs one showroom and a website, about sixty pieces in the catalogue. Online shoppers keep asking the same question in different words: will this sofa fit my living room? She starts researching AR, and one name keeps showing up next to the big brands. Tangiblee, the enterprise platform behind immersive shopping experiences for companies like Fossil, MCM, Victorinox, and Home24. Then she finds ARQR360. Both promise true-to-size AR for furniture. Both sound right. Neither website speaks to a store like hers directly.

This is the honest version.

What Tangiblee is

Tangiblee is an enterprise AR and immersive shopping platform. It has been refining its process since 2014, starting with Techstars, and it now works with some of the largest retailers in the world. Its pitch is scale: a human-in-the-loop system that its CEO says can process thousands of SKUs a day, turning a brand's existing product imagery into interactive experiences, virtual try-on, and lifestyle content.

The key word is enterprise. Tangiblee sells to brands and large retailers that run big catalogues across teams. Its furniture work shows up through names like Home24, the big European online furniture retailer. When a platform serves companies at that size, the onboarding, the contract, and the pricing all take the shape of an enterprise project. That is who it is built for.

What ARQR360 is

ARQR360 is a done-for-you AR (Augmented Reality) catalogue for independent furniture retailers. You send photos of your bestselling pieces and their real listed dimensions. We build the true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views, host them, and hand you links and QR codes that open the view in your customer's phone browser. No app. No platform to learn. No 3D team to hire.

The audience is different. ARQR360 is built for stores that sell finished pieces off the floor, not brands managing thousands of SKUs across a product team.

Price: enterprise quote vs one public flat rate

Tangiblee does not publish pricing on its website. Software directories list no starting price, no free version, and no free trial. That is normal for an enterprise platform: you describe your catalogue and your goals, and you get a quote. The only reliable way to know the number is to ask in writing.

ARQR360 publishes its price: $199 one-time setup and $19 a month, locked for life for the first 50 stores. AR view production is included. There is no product cap, no per-view fee, and no content creation surcharge sitting on top.

Setup effort: a catalogue project vs sending photos

Tangiblee's strength is processing volume. Its team and its system turn your product imagery into interactive experiences at a pace built for catalogues that count in the thousands. If you run a brand with a big range and a digital team, that is the right machinery. For a store with sixty pieces and two people who also run the floor, the machinery is bigger than the job.

With ARQR360 the project is: send photos, get a working AR catalogue back in about seven days. The links go on your product pages next to the price. The QR codes go on the tags in the showroom. There is nothing to integrate and nothing to maintain.

True-to-size accuracy

Both routes show the piece at its real dimensions. The honest difference is not the render. It is what your store has to go through to get a true-to-size view of your bestselling sofa live this month, and what you pay every year to keep it there. A store whose problem is shoppers guessing whether a sectional fits their living room needs the view live, not a perfect platform roadmap.

One more number, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Whichever route you take, giving shoppers a true-to-size view beats a flat photo.
