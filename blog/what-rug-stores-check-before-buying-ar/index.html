What a Rug Store Should Check Before Buying AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Leeds. She sells hand knotted rugs, Persian rugs, and modern wool rugs from one shop and a website. Her customers love her rugs in the shop, but online they hesitate. A rug is the hardest thing to buy from a photo. It has to fit the room, suit the floor, and work with the light, and a photo answers none of that. So she starts looking at AR (Augmented Reality) providers, and every one of them sounds good on its own website.

They are not all the same. A bad AR view is worse than no AR view at all, because a shopper who sees the wrong size in her own room will blame the rug, not the provider. Here are the checks that matter, in the order a rug store should run them.

Check 1: is the size true, or just close

This is the check that decides everything else. A rug view must be built from the real listed dimensions of your rug, so the size on screen is the size that arrives at the door. Ask the provider how they set the size. If the answer is vague, or if they talk about approximate scaling, walk away. A rug that is five centimetres out in AR is a rug that comes back.

Our designing department builds each view from the real listed dimensions, so a 200 by 300 rug shows as 200 by 300 on the shopper's floor. Before you sign, ask for a live test of your own bestselling rug and measure what you see against the real piece. That one test tells you more than any sales page.

Check 2: does the shopper need an app

Most shoppers will not install an app to look at a rug. If the provider's AR only works inside an app, most of your buyers will never see it. Ask for a plain link and a QR code that opens the view in the phone browser. No install, no account, no waiting.

With ARQR360 each rug gets a link and a QR code. No programming, no changes to your website needed. We send you a QR code for each product. Try it on your own phone before you buy anything. If it takes more than a tap, it is too slow.

Check 3: who builds the views, and what do they need from you

Some providers hand you software and expect you to build the views yourself, or they ask you to supply finished 3D files you do not have. A rug store should not need a 3D team. Ask who does the building. The right answer is that the provider's own designing department builds every view from your photos and your listed dimensions, and you change nothing about how you already sell.

Ask for a real date, not a vague promise. A provider that takes months to launch your first rugs is a provider that will be slow every time you add stock.
