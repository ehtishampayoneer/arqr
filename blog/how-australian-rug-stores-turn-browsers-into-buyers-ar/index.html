How Australian Rug Stores Turn Browsers Into Buyers with True-to-Size AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Melbourne. Her website gets steady traffic. People browse the rugs, zoom in on the patterns, read the size guide, and leave. The rugs are good. The prices are fair. The problem is not the product. The problem is the question every online rug shopper asks and cannot answer from a photo: will this rug look right in my room, at this size, on my floor.

That unanswered question is where most rug sales are lost. A shopper who cannot picture the rug in their own room does not take a chance on a large, heavy, expensive piece. They close the tab and keep browsing. AR (Augmented Reality) answers the question directly. The shopper points their phone at their own floor and sees the rug there, at its true size, in their own light.

Why browsers leave without buying

A rug is not a small purchase. It anchors a room. Get the size wrong and the whole room looks off, and sending a rug back is a hassle nobody wants. So shoppers do the sensible thing. They hesitate. They measure the room again. They ask a friend. They put it off until the weekend. Then life gets in the way and the sale never happens.

Photos cannot fix this, because a photo shows the rug, not the rug in your room. A size chart helps, but numbers on a screen do not feel like a rug under a coffee table. The shopper needs to see it, in place, before they commit. That is the gap between browsing and buying, and it is a size confidence gap, not a taste gap.

What the shopper sees with AR (Augmented Reality)

The shopper taps a link on the product page. No app to download. The rug appears on their own floor, at its true size, through the phone camera. They walk around it. They check whether it reaches under the sofa the way it should. They try the next size up and see at once that it crowds the doorway. They go back to the size that fits and buy it.

This is why it works for rugs more than for most products. A rug lives on the floor, and the floor is right there in front of the shopper. The check takes seconds, and it answers the one question that was stopping the order. Shoppers who can check the size hesitate less, and shoppers who hesitate less buy more often. Shopify reports that products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. That is Shopify's own data, not ours, but the reason behind it is plain: confidence sells.

Where the view has to sit

  • Next to the price, on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters, not three clicks later.
  • On every size option. A rug that comes in four sizes needs four views. Shoppers compare sizes by seeing them, not by reading numbers.
  • In the shop, on the price tag. A QR code on the tag opens the same rug in AR (Augmented Reality). A customer who liked a rug in store can check it at home that evening and order from the sofa.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your shoppers browse.
