How Australian Rug Stores Help Shoppers Pick the Right Rug Size for Open Plan Living with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Brisbane. She sells wool rugs, jute rugs and flatweaves from one shop and a website that ships across the country. Her hardest sale is not the colour or the pattern. It is the size. Her shoppers live in open plan homes, one big room for lounging, eating and walking through, with no walls to judge against. On a product page, a rug looks like a rectangle. In an open plan room, it has to do a job: mark where the living zone ends and the dining zone begins.

Get the size wrong and the room tells on it straight away. A rug that is too small floats in the middle of the floor, with the sofa legs off it and the coffee table half on it. A rug that is too big creeps under the dining chairs and catches every chair leg. Either way, the rug goes back, or the shopper never orders at all because they could not picture it. The photo cannot answer the question. Only the room can.

Why open plan sizing is its own problem

In a closed room, the walls do half the sizing work. The shopper measures wall to wall and picks the rug that fills the space between them. In open plan living, there are no walls to measure against. The shopper has to judge a zone, not a room: how far the rug should run past the front legs of the sofa, how much bare floor should sit between the rug edge and the dining table, and where one zone ends so the next one can start. A tape measure gives numbers. It does not give the picture.

This is also why open plan shoppers hesitate longer than other rug buyers. They are not just buying a rug. They are deciding how their biggest room will feel. A shopper who cannot see it will either order the safe middle size, which is often the wrong one, or close the tab and keep looking. The store loses the sale without ever knowing the shopper was close to buying.

What a true size view changes

With AR (Augmented Reality), the shopper taps a link on the product page and points their phone at their own floor. The rug appears at its true size, in their own room, in the light of their own home. They walk around it. They check whether the front legs of the sofa sit on it. They check whether the dining chairs clear the edge. They try the next size up, then the next size down, and the difference is right in front of them instead of in their head.

That check matters most for the shopper who lives far from the shop. A customer in Perth cannot pop into a Brisbane store to unroll a rug on a shop floor and stand back. A customer in a regional town cannot compare three sizes side by side. The AR (Augmented Reality) view brings the shop floor to them, on the phone they are already holding, in the room the rug would actually live in. No app to download. The view opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code.

The three size checks every open plan shopper should run

First, the sofa check. The front legs of the sofa should sit on the rug, with the rug running past them by a comfortable margin. If the rug stops short of the sofa, the seating zone looks unfinished, and the rug will slide every time someone sits down.

Second, the walkway check. Leave bare floor between the rug edge and the next zone, so the room still reads as separate spaces. A rug that runs right up to the dining table merges two zones into one big carpeted blur, which is the opposite of what open plan living is meant to feel like.

Third, the dining check. Pull the dining chairs out as if someone were sitting down to eat. If the back legs land off the rug, the chairs will catch and wobble, and the rug will wear at the edge. A true size view shows this in seconds. A size chart never does.

Where the view belongs

First, next to the size selector on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see it at true size link beside the sizes answers the question at the moment it matters, not after the shopper has already closed the tab.

Second, in social media posts and stories. Australian shoppers browse on phones, and phones run the AR (Augmented Reality) view. A link in a post opens the same rug in the same phone the shopper is already holding.

Third, in the shop itself. A QR code on the price tag lets a visitor take the rug home in their pocket. They check it against their own floor that evening, show it to their family, and order from home. The visit starts the sale. The evening finishes it.
