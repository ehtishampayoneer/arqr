ARQR360 vs Nextech3D ARitize 3D: What a Furniture Store Actually Gets from Each

Picture a furniture store owner in Manchester. She sells mid-century armchairs and walnut sideboards from one showroom and a website that ships across the UK. Her customers keep asking the same question on the phone: will this chair fit in my living room. She decides AR is the answer. Two names keep coming up: Nextech3D and ARQR360. Both promise true-to-size AR. Both serve ecommerce. But they are built for different kinds of stores. Here is the honest version.

This is written for a store owner, not a developer.

What Nextech3D ARitize 3D is

Nextech3D is a Canadian technology company. Its ARitize 3D platform turns a retailer's existing 2D product images into 3D and AR views using AI. The flow is platform based: you install their app on Shopify or WooCommerce, start 3D creation for your products, and publish the views to your product pages. Shoppers can spin the piece and drop it into their own room with AR.

The big idea is automation. In the company's own words, their AI turns your existing 2D product images into high-quality 3D and AR experiences. No 3D artist needed for every product. That is a real saving if you have hundreds of products and the staff to run the queue.

What ARQR360 is

ARQR360 is a done-for-you AR (Augmented Reality) service for independent furniture retailers. You send photos of your bestselling pieces with their real listed dimensions, and we build true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views from them, host them, and hand you links and QR codes that open in the customer's phone browser. No app. No platform to run. No work on your side.

The audience is different. ARQR360 is built for stores that sell finished pieces off the shelf, not brands running a platform project across a catalogue team.

Price: platform pricing vs one public flat rate

Nextech3D does not publish one flat public price list for furniture stores. The pricing is platform based and depends on your catalogue, so you talk to their team and get a quote. In the company's own public filings they described 3D content creation as included in monthly hosting fees, and a 2020 early-access statement quoted $40 a month per product for hosting. Treat those as history, not today's price. Ask for a written quote for your catalogue size.

ARQR360 publishes its price: $199 one-time setup and $19 a month, locked for life for the first 50 stores. View building is included. No product cap, no per-view fee.

Setup effort: install and run a platform vs send photos

With ARitize 3D you run the process yourself inside their apps: install the Shopify or WooCommerce app, start creation for each product, and publish the views to your pages. The company presents this as three simple steps. It is simple if your store already runs on one of those platforms and someone on your side can review the results and keep the views working. It is still your project.

With ARQR360 there is no project. You send photos and real dimensions, and about seven days later your pieces are live as links and QR codes. Nothing to install, nothing to maintain.

True-to-size accuracy

Both show products at real dimensions in AR. The honest difference is what happens when a model is off. In an automated pipeline the model comes out of your photos and needs your eyes on it before it goes live. If the camera lens distorted the shot or the listed dimensions were rounded, the AR view will carry that error. With a done-for-you service, a person checks the view against your listed dimensions before it reaches your customers.

For a store whose problem is customers guessing whether a chair fits, a true-to-size view that is checked before it ships beats a fast pipeline nobody reviewed. One more number, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Whichever route you take, a true-to-size view beats a flat photo.

Who each one fits

Nextech3D ARitize 3D makes sense if:

  • You run your store on Shopify or WooCommerce
  • You have hundreds of products and want a self service pipeline
  • Someone on your side can review the views and manage the pages
  • Your budget fits platform pricing quoted per catalogue

ARQR360 is designed for stores like yours if:

  • You are an independent furniture retailer or showroom
  • You hold no CAD files and no 3D team
  • You want your pieces live this month with no new work on your side
  • You need one public price with no surprises

The question to ask both vendors

Before you sign anything, ask both of us the same four questions in writing: what is the full annual cost including view creation, who builds the views and from what source material, what happens to the views if you end the contract, and does the customer need to download an app. The answers will tell you which tool fits your store faster than any feature list.
