How US Decor Stores Win First-Time Buyers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Austin. She sells wall art, mirrors, vases and lamps from one shop and a website. Her regular customers buy fast. They know her range and they trust her eye. Her first-time buyers are a different story. They have never bought decor online. They love a piece on her website, and then they stop. They have no idea if it will suit their room, and they have no one to ask.

That first-time buyer is most of the growth in US decor sales. She is setting up her first home, or refreshing the room she has had for years. She is not comparing your mirror with five others. She is deciding whether she can trust buying decor from a screen at all. Here is how a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page wins her trust, and her order.

Why first-time buyers hesitate

A decor piece is a real purchase for someone who has never bought one online. The questions in her head are simple, and a photo cannot answer any of them.

First, will it fit the wall. A mirror that looks right in your bright shop can swallow a small wall at home, or sit too small above the sideboard and look lost. She has never measured for wall art before, and numbers on a listing do not help her picture it. Second, will the colour work in her light. A vase that glows warm in your shop window can look cold in her living room, or clash with the sofa she already owns. Third, will it suit the room. A bold print looks great in a photo and can take over a small room in real life, and a quiet piece can vanish against a dark wall.

She does not want to get it wrong. A mirror is not a small thing to send back. It is heavy, it costs real money to ship across the country, and the return feels like a failure. So she browses, she hesitates, and she buys nothing. Or she drives to a big shop in town, where she can see the piece on the wall, even if it is not the piece she wanted.

What changes when she sees it at true size

A true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view answers all three questions in about a minute, on her own phone, in her own room. She taps the link on the product page, points her phone at her wall, and the piece appears at its true size, right where it would hang. She steps back. She checks it against her sofa. She sees the colour in her own light, next to her own furniture.

Our designing department builds each view from the real listed dimensions of the piece, so the size on screen is the size that arrives at the door. That is what turns a nervous first-time buyer into a confident one. She stops asking if it will suit the room and starts asking when it will arrive. One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and first-time buyers hesitate most of all, because they have never done this before.

Where the view goes on your site

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where the size doubt lives. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your first-time buyers browse.
