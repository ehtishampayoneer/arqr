How Canadian Decor Stores Cut Wrong-Colour Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Toronto. She sells lamps, mirrors, framed art and vases online to shoppers all over Canada. A lamp comes back. The customer loved it on the screen. In her living room it looked the wrong colour, dull yellow against her grey sofa, nothing like the soft brass on the product page. The store pays the return shipping, checks the lamp, repacks it, and puts it back on the shelf. The sale is gone. The cost stays.

Returns like this are the quiet cost of selling decor online in Canada. They rarely come from damage or a broken piece. They come from colour. The shopper guessed, and the guess was wrong.

Why colour is the hardest thing to sell from a photo

A photo shows the piece in someone else's room, under someone else's light. The shopper's room is different. Her bulbs are warm. Her window faces north. Her walls are grey, or cream, or deep green. In a Canadian winter the light through the window is thin and cool for months, and a brass lamp that glowed in the studio photo can read flat yellow on her side table. A rug that looked warm beige on the screen can read cold grey on her floor.

The shopper knows this. She zooms in on the photo, reads the colour name, checks the dimensions, and still has to guess. Most of the time the guess is close enough and she keeps the piece. Often enough it is not, and the piece comes back. Each return costs the store shipping both ways, the time to check and repack, and days when the piece sits unsold. A few returns a month add up to real money, and a returned customer rarely buys again.

What changes when she checks it in her own room

AR (Augmented Reality) moves the check from after delivery to before buying. The shopper points her phone at her own side table and the lamp appears there, at its true size, in the light of her own room, next to her own sofa. She checks the mirror above the mantel. She holds the framed print above the sofa she already owns. The check takes ten seconds, and the colour surprise is gone before checkout.

That is the whole mechanism. A photo asks the shopper to imagine the colour in her room. An AR (Augmented Reality) view asks her to look at it there. When she can see the piece where it will actually live, the question changes from will the colour work to when can I have it.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size and the true colour hesitate less, and shoppers who hesitate less buy more and return less.

Where the colour check belongs

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where the buying decision happens. A see it in your room link beside the buy button answers the colour question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. Customers check it at home against their own walls and come back decided.
  • In your social posts. A short clip of a shopper checking a mirror in her own room shows the idea faster than any paragraph. The viewer sees the check happen and wants the same certainty.
