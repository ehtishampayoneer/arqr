The Best AR Tools for Furniture Stores in 2026

Picture a furniture store owner in Chicago. She sells sofas, armchairs, and dining tables from one showroom and a website that ships across the Midwest. Her product pages get steady traffic. Visitors zoom in on the photos, check the dimensions, then close the tab. The pieces sit unsold.

Furniture is the category where size kills the sale. A 220cm sofa and a 180cm one look identical in a square product photo, and the wrong guess comes back on a truck. True-to-size AR answers the one question photos cannot: the customer points their phone at their floor and sees the piece in their own room, at its real size. Here are the real options a furniture store can buy in 2026, written for a store owner, not a developer.

Option 1: IKEA Kreativ

IKEA Kreativ is IKEA's own room-design app. Shoppers scan their room, drop in IKEA furniture, and see how it looks. It is free, it is good at what it does, and it belongs to IKEA. Your store cannot put its own sofas on its own product pages with it. We compared the two routes in ARQR360 vs IKEA Kreativ for furniture stores.

Good when: you shop at IKEA and want to plan a room. Bad when: you run a furniture store and want AR on your own product pages. It is a shopper app for one retailer's catalogue, not a tool your store can buy.

Option 2: Shopify AR viewer apps

If you sell on Shopify, AR viewer apps on the app store add a 3D viewer to your product pages for a small monthly subscription. Public listings put pricing around free to roughly $36 a month, depending on how many products you publish.

The catch is the same one every viewer app has. The apps are viewers only. You supply every 3D model yourself, usually commissioned from 3D artists at roughly $100 to $500 per product. A furniture store with 120 pieces is looking at a modelling bill in the tens of thousands before the first view loads. The subscription is small. The modelling bill is the project. We worked the same maths in our pricing breakdown for furniture stores.

Good when: you already have 3D models of your pieces, or a designer on staff who makes them. Bad when: you do not, which is almost every small furniture store.

Option 3: 3D and AR platforms

Platforms like Threekit, Cylindo, and Marxent sell 3D configuration and AR as enterprise software. You get serious tooling: product configurators, 3D viewers, AR views, and asset management across a whole catalogue. We have compared several of them for furniture stores: Cylindo, Marxent, and Plattar.

Good when: you are a brand or a large retailer with a digital team and a budget sized for enterprise software. Bad when: you are a small store. These are platforms sold to brands, not plug-and-play products for a 40-sofa showroom. Pricing is quote-based, so get a written quote for your own catalogue before you compare.

The price is flat and public: $199 one-time setup and $19 a month, locked for life for the first 50 stores, with every AR (Augmented Reality) view built and hosted as part of it. No product cap, no per-view fees.

Good when: you want AR live this month with no new hires. Bad when: you need deep custom work like a full product configurator. That is a platform job, not a service job.

What to check before you choose

  • True-to-size proof: for furniture, size is the entire question. A sofa even slightly off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted. Ask every provider how they verify scale against the real piece.
  • The app question: if your customer must install an app, most of them will not. Browser AR gets used. App AR gets skipped.
  • Who makes the models: the biggest cost on most routes is model creation, not the software. Ask whether the views are built for you and what new season stock costs.
  • Total cost, not the monthly fee: add the models, the setup, and the new range. Per-model fees quietly dwarf subscriptions on catalogues that change.

Where each option lands

If you are a brand with a digital team and an enterprise budget, a 3D platform is a serious option with the configurator tooling to show for it. If you already have 3D models and a Shopify store, a viewer app is a cheap add. If you have neither, a done-for-you service is the route that ends with AR actually live on your site.

One more number, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Whichever route you take, giving shoppers a true-to-size view beats a flat photo.
