What US Rug Stores Should Look for in an AR (Augmented Reality) Provider

Picture a rug store owner in Dallas. She sells wool rugs, hand knotted rugs and flatweaves online and from one showroom. Most of her website visitors live in other states. They browse the photos, wonder if the rug will fit under their sofa, and leave. She starts looking for an AR (Augmented Reality) provider, and every website sounds the same. Here is what to actually look for, in plain terms, before you sign anything.

1. True size, proven against your real dimensions

Start here, because size is the whole question for rugs. A rug that is even a few inches off teaches the shopper the tool cannot be trusted. Look for a provider who checks every view against the real listed dimensions of your rugs, and who can say plainly what happens when a rug is off. A serious answer names a process. A vague answer is the answer.

Look at pattern scale too. A rug is not just a rectangle. The shopper needs to see the pattern at its real scale, not stretched or shrunk to fill a space. If the border looks wrong on screen, the rug will look wrong in the room, and the order comes back.

2. No app for the shopper

Look for a view that opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code. If the customer must download an app to see your rug, most of them will not. People do not install apps to look at rugs. A browser view gets used. Test that on your own phone with our live rug demo. It opens in the browser with no install.

3. A provider who does the work for you

Look at who produces the views, and what they start from. On some routes, you supply the files yourself and manage the whole pipeline. That is a project, not a purchase, and it suits a store with a digital team. On the done for you route, you send photos and your real listed dimensions, and the provider produces the views.

4. The full one year price, in writing

Look for a quote for your full catalogue, for one year, with everything included. The monthly fee is usually the smallest line. The rest is per product production fees, per view charges that grow with your traffic, setup fees, and the cost of new season stock. A quote that will not put that number in writing is a quote you cannot compare.

5. Support after launch

Look for a provider who stays after the views go live. Who checks that the links still work when you add new rugs. Who fixes a view when a listing changes. Who answers when a shopper says the rug looked different on their phone. A provider that disappears after launch leaves you holding a catalogue you cannot maintain.

6. Where the links go

  • Next to the size on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see it at true size link beside the dimensions answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the swing tag in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same rug in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your shoppers browse.
