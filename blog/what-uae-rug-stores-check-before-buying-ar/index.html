What UAE Rug Stores Should Check Before Buying AR

Picture a rug store owner in Dubai. She stocks hand-knotted Persians, modern kilims, and oversized area rugs from workshops she trusts. Her website photos are shot well. The dimensions are listed. And still, every week a few rugs come back with the same complaint. The rug looked bigger in the photos. Or the pattern swallowed the room. Or the colour sat wrong against the marble floor.

For a rug, size is the whole product. A rug that is off by 20 centimeters changes which furniture it fits under. A pattern photographed from above behaves differently on a floor. A colour picked under showroom spots reads different in Gulf daylight through a tall window. A photo cannot carry any of that. True-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views can. Her customer points a phone at the living room floor and sees the rug at its real size, in the room it will live in, before they buy.

The question she asked us was simple: what should I check before I buy this? This is the checklist we gave her.

1. True size, measured to the centimeter

An AR view that is even slightly off teaches the shopper the tool cannot be trusted. Ask the provider how they check scale. The answer you want: every rug is produced from its real listed dimensions, and the scale is checked before anything ships. Our designing department works exactly that way. They build each AR (Augmented Reality) view from your product photos plus the real dimensions, and nothing goes live until the size matches.

2. Colours you can trust

Rug dyes shift between showroom light and home light, and a phone screen adds its own shift. Ask the provider what photos they build from. The honest answer: your own product photos, and they flag it when a photo is too far off to trust. A provider that builds from your real catalogue photos is showing your rugs. A provider that works from stock images or generic renders is showing something else.

3. No app for the shopper

If the customer has to install an app to check a rug, most of them never will. Ask where the AR view opens. The answer you want: a link and a QR code that open in the phone browser. Ours do exactly that. They work on the product page, in a WhatsApp chat, on a shelf tag in the showroom, on a printed flyer. Try the live rug demo on your own phone and time it.

4. The whole catalogue, not just five pieces

Five AR views will not change your returns. Eighty might. Ask the provider how they handle a full range, and how long the first batch takes. This is a done-for-you question. You send photos of your bestsellers and their dimensions. Our designing department builds the AR views, checks each one, and hands you the links and QR codes. About seven days from photos to a working catalogue.

5. Follow-ups when your stock changes

Rug ranges change. New pieces arrive, sold ones leave. Ask what happens after launch. With ARQR360, the follow-ups are part of the service. You send photos of the new arrivals, we build their views and add them to your catalogue. You do not hire anyone, brief anyone, or manage a project. That ongoing relationship is why stores stay, so ask for it by name before you sign anything.

6. A custom package if you need one

Some stores want more than product links. A showroom kiosk where customers browse the full range in AR. Branded pages that carry the store's name and look. Ask the provider if they build custom. We do, and the free test view is the same offer either way: one of your rugs, free, on your phone, before you decide.
