How a UK Shoe Store Gets Its First Free AR View

Picture a shoe store owner in Leeds. She sells trainers and leather shoes from one high-street shop and a website. Her most expensive problem is not rent. It is the wrong-size order. The shoe goes out, comes back, and she pays the postage both ways. Half the returns arrive scuffed and cannot go back on the shelf at full price. She has heard AR can help, but every version she has seen looked like a software project. She has no developer, no 3D team, and no appetite for either.

Here is the cheapest way for a store like hers to find out if AR works. It costs nothing, takes one photo, and puts the answer on her own phone.

Why one free view beats a sales call

A sales call tells you what AR could do. A free view shows you what it does with your shoe. There is a big difference. On a call, everything is a demo of someone else's product. On your phone, it is your bestselling trainer, at its true size, next to your own foot. You can check it against the real shoe in about ten seconds. If the size is wrong, you know immediately. If it is right, you know something a brochure cannot tell you.

That is why the first view is free. It is not a sample of the idea. It is the idea, working, with your product.

What you send: one photo and the real dimensions

Start with your bestselling shoe. Not the whole catalogue. One pair. Send a photo of it, the kind your website already shows, plus the real listed dimensions. That is the whole brief. No special photography, no 3D scanning, no studio setup.

Our designing department takes it from there. They produce a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view of the shoe from your photo and dimensions, and they check the scale against the real measurements before anything ships. A view that is even slightly off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted, so the size check happens before you ever see it.

What comes back: a link and a QR code

About seven days later you get two things. A link for the product page, and a QR code for everything else. Both open the AR view in the phone browser. No app. Not for you, not for your customer.

The no-app part matters more than it sounds. If your customer had to install an app to check a shoe size, most of them never would. People do not install apps to look at trainers. A link that opens in the browser in seconds gets used. Try the live footwear demo on your own phone and time it.

How to check it yourself in ten seconds

When the free view arrives, do this. Open the link on your phone. Hold the phone next to the real shoe on your counter. The AR shoe should sit at exactly the same size as the one in your hand. Then point it at your own foot. You get a real size idea of the shoe against the one foot you know best.

One honest limit, and every shoe store should hear it plainly. AR shows true size, not fit. No phone screen tells a shopper whether a shoe pinches or rubs. What it does well is stop the bracketing habit: the shopper who was going to order two sizes and return one orders the size that looks right against their own foot. Fewer double shipments. Fewer repacks. The sale lands, and it stays landed.

Where the link and QR code go

The link belongs on the product page, next to the size selector. That is where the size decision happens, and a see-it-at-true-size link answers the question at the moment of doubt. The QR code goes everywhere the shoe goes: on the shelf tag in the shop, on the shoe box, in your Instagram posts, on a printed flyer in the window. A customer browsing at 10pm, when your shop is closed and your website is open, can check the size and buy at 10pm.
