How European Home Decor Stores Cut Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a home decor store owner in Amsterdam. She sells wall art, mirrors, vases and lamps from one shop and a website. Her returns tell the same story every month. The pieces come back because of size. The canvas looked bigger online. The mirror swallowed the hallway. The vase did not fit the shelf.

This is the quietest cost in home decor retail. Nobody talks about it at trade shows. A returned mirror costs shipping twice and sells used the second time. The fix is simple. Let the shopper see the piece at its true size before they order.

Where the guessing happens

Decor is the hardest category to judge online. A sofa has listed dimensions that a buyer can measure against a room. A wall art print has them too, but almost nobody measures. The buyer looks at a photo, feels the size, and hopes. Mirrors and lighting are worse. They interact with the room, and a photo of a product on white cannot show that.

Picture a buyer in Lyon. She orders two framed prints for the wall above her sideboard. They arrive. They look lost. She packs them up and returns both. Nobody lied to her. The listing had the sizes. She just could not picture them. That gap between listed size and pictured size is where most decor returns come from.

What the shopper actually checks

AR (Augmented Reality) answers one question. How does this look in my room, at its true size. The shopper points their phone at their wall or their shelf. The piece appears where it would really stand. Too big is obvious. Too small is obvious. The guessing ends.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less and return less.
