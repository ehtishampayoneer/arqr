How Canadian Decor Stores Turn Browsers Into Buyers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Toronto. She sells wall art, table lamps, mirrors, and vases from one shop and a website. Her product pages get plenty of visits. The visits are not the problem. The problem is how many of them leave without buying.

Most of those visitors are not saying no. They are saying not yet. They like the lamp, but they cannot tell if it will suit their side table, or if the wall art will overwhelm the wall above the sofa. So they close the page and promise themselves they will measure later. Most of them never come back.

Why decor shoppers browse and leave

A decor item lives in a room, next to things the shopper already owns. A lamp has to sit right on the table. A mirror has to fit the wall and reflect the right light. A vase has to hold its own against the furniture around it. A photo shows the design, but it does not show what a 90 centimetre wide framed print looks like above the shopper's own sofa, in the light of their own room.

Size charts list numbers, and numbers do not settle it. The shopper has to picture the item in a room they are not standing in. Some guess and buy, and some of those guesses come back as returns. Most do not guess at all. They leave, and the store never learns why.

The fix is not more traffic. The traffic is already there. The fix is giving the traffic a reason to trust what they see before they buy.

What changes when the shopper checks it at home first

AR (Augmented Reality) lets the shopper point their phone at their own room and see the item there at its true size, in the light of that room. They check whether the lamp leaves enough space on the side table. They check whether the wall art fits between the window and the door. They see the colour against their own wall before they spend a dollar.

That is the whole mechanism. It is not a fancy effect. It is the item, at its true size, in the shopper's own room. If the view gets the size and colour right, the shopper trusts it, and a trusting shopper buys. For the returns side of the same story, read how Canadian decor stores cut returns with AR.

Three places the AR view turns a browser into a buyer

First, the product page. The AR link sits next to the price, where the buying decision happens. A shopper who can check the size in ten seconds does not need to save the page for later. They buy now, while they are still looking at it.

Second, the shop floor. A QR code on the shelf tag opens the same item in AR, so a customer browsing in the store can check it at home that evening and order from the sofa. The shop keeps selling after the customer leaves, and the customer arrives already sure.

Third, the evening. Decor browsing peaks after the shops close. A shopper looking at 9pm can check the lamp in their own room right then, from the product page, and buy at 9pm. A store with AR on every product page sells while the shop is dark.

What to ask before you buy AR for your decor store

Ask the provider how they prove true size. A serious answer names a process: every view checked against the real listed dimensions of the item. A vague answer is a warning.

Ask whether the shopper needs an app. If the answer is yes, most of your customers will never use it. People do not install apps to check a lamp. A view that opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code, is the version that actually gets used.
