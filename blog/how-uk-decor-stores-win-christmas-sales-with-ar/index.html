How UK Decor Stores Win Christmas Sales with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in York. She sells table lamps, mirrors, vases and wall art from one shop and a website. November arrives and the gift shoppers arrive with it. They browse her product pages in the evening, find a lamp they like, and stop. They cannot tell if it will suit the room it is meant for, because the room is not theirs. It belongs to the person getting the gift.

That shopper is not saying no. She is saying she cannot tell yet. At Christmas the doubt is doubled. She is buying for someone else's home, from a photo, with no way to measure. Some of those shoppers buy anyway and guess wrong. Those gifts come back in January. The rest never buy at all. Both cost the store, and both are avoidable.

Why Christmas decor is hard to buy from a photo

A photo of a lamp on a white background gives the shopper nothing to measure against. No table, no wall, no familiar object. A mirror looks the same at 40cm and 80cm in a square photo. A vase could be a desk piece or a floor piece. The shopper reads the dimensions, tries to picture them, and gives up.

Gift buying makes it worse. The giver cannot walk into the recipient's living room with a tape measure. She is guessing twice: the taste and the size. When the size guess fails, the return lands in January, right when the store is quietest. A return in January costs freight both ways and a sale that looked safe in December.

What the shopper actually checks

AR (Augmented Reality) answers one question. How does this piece look at its true size, in a real room? The shopper points their phone at their own wall or side table. The lamp appears at its true size, so they can check it against furniture they already own. A mirror appears on the wall at its real width. A vase sits on the shelf at its real height. That check ends the guessing that stops so many Christmas orders.

That check works best on the pieces that already get traffic. Bestsellers first. Twenty proven pieces covering most of your Christmas revenue teach you what shoppers actually do with the views, before you cover the whole catalogue.

Why the browser view gets used

Ask one question before you buy any AR route: does the shopper need to download an app? If the answer is yes, most of them will not use it. People do not install apps to check a lamp. A view that opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code, gets used. Test that on your own phone with our live decor demo.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and at Christmas hesitation is a lost sale.

Where the links go

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided, or send the link to the person they are buying for.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your Christmas shoppers browse.
