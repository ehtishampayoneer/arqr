How UK Shoe Stores Answer the Christmas Sizing Rush With True-To-Size AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a shoe store owner in Leeds. She sells boots, trainers, and party heels from one shop and a website. Every December her sales double. Every January her returns do too.

The problem is not quality. It is sizing, guessed wrong. A daughter buys boots for her mum in the wrong size. A husband picks trainers for his wife from a size chart. By January the post room is full of boxes coming back.

Why shoes are the hardest gift to get right

Shoes are personal in a way a scarf is not. Half a size off and they sit in the box. Size charts disagree between brands, and UK sizing does not map neatly to EU numbers. Gift buyers cannot ask the person, or the surprise is gone. So they guess.

Some guess from the recipient's last pair. Some order two sizes and return one. Most just hope. The store pays for the hope in January: return postage, re-boxing, and stock that missed the Christmas rush.

What the AR view does

AR (Augmented Reality) shows the shoe at its real size. The gift buyer opens the product page on their phone, taps the link, and the boot appears at its true dimensions. They can hold it against the recipient's own shoe, or against their own foot, and see whether the size they are buying is the right size. Try the live footwear demo on your own phone. It opens in the browser. No app to install.

One honest note

AR (Augmented Reality) cannot tell you how a shoe feels. Comfort is for trying on. What it can do is give you the true size: the length, the width, the real size idea, before you buy. That is what gift buyers guess at, and it is the part that causes most January returns.
