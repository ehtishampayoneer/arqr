How Canadian Shoe Stores Cut Their January Returns with True-Size AR

Every January, Canadian shoe stores face the same pile. Holiday orders come back through the door. Boots that looked right on the screen and wrong on the foot. Two sizes ordered, one kept, one returned. A pair worn once outside that can never be sold again.

Picture a shopper in Toronto. She orders winter boots in her usual size. The photo looked great. The size chart said eight. But her foot is a little wide, and this brand runs narrow. The boots arrive, they pinch, and back they go. She is not angry at the store. She guessed, and the guess was wrong.

Most January returns are not quality problems. They are size guesses that missed.

Why winter shoes come back more than anything else

Boots and winter shoes are the hardest size guess in the store. Thick socks change how a boot feels. Brands size differently. A shopper buying online cannot try anything on, so they do the sensible thing. They order two sizes and return one. Or they order one size and hope.

Hope is expensive. Every return costs shipping both ways, staff time to inspect and restock, and sometimes a pair that can never be sold as new again. January is when all of it lands at once.

What changes when shoppers see the true size first

True-size AR (Augmented Reality) lets the shopper hold up their phone and see the boot at its real listed size, right next to their own foot. Not a stylised spin. The actual length and width, on their own floor, in their own hallway light.

That one moment answers the question the size chart cannot: does this look right against my foot. The shopper who sees the boot is clearly shorter than their foot picks the next size up before ordering, not after. The one who sees it is far too roomy tries another style instead of ordering and returning.

Shopify's own reported data says products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better. That is their number, not ours. For shoe stores the quieter win sits in the returns pile. Fewer size guesses means fewer January parcels coming back through the door.
