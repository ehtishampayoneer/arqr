How Australian Shoe Stores Cut Size Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a shoe store owner in Melbourne. She stocks runners, boots and sandals from a dozen brands. The product photos are beautiful. The size chart is exact. And still, a big share of her online orders come back with a size change request. The customer loved the shoe. They just ordered the wrong size.

This is bracketing, and shoe stores live with it more than almost any other retailer. A 40 in one brand is not a 40 in another. Some brands run long, some run narrow, some drift between batches. The shopper knows this from experience, so they order two sizes and return the one that does not work. The store pays for both shipments. The warehouse repacks the returned pair. The margin on that sale is gone before it lands.

Why photos and size charts cannot fix this

Three problems work against every shoe store. First, brand sizing drifts. A chart tells you the foot length for each size, but it says nothing about the shoe's actual proportions, and very few shoppers measure their feet with a ruler. Second, style changes the shape. A pointed toe and a round toe both labelled a 38 look completely different next to your own foot, yet online they sit in the same row of the chart. Third, there is no reference point. A photo of a boot gives no sense of the space it takes up. Numbers on a chart do not turn into a real size idea.

What a true-to-size AR view does

A true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view puts the shoe at its real dimensions in the shopper's phone view. They hold the phone over their own foot and see the boot at true size beside it. It does not tell them how the shoe feels, and no honest store should claim that. It gives them something photos cannot: a real size idea of the actual product, in scale with the one foot they know.

For the store this attacks bracketing at its root. The shopper who was going to order two sizes orders the one that looks right against their own foot. Fewer double shipments. Fewer repacks. The sale lands, and it stays landed.

Where the links go

  • On the product page, beside the size selector. That is where the size decision happens. A see-it-at-true-size link next to the dropdown answers the question at the moment of doubt.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where Australian shoppers browse shoe stores.
  • On the shelf edge in the shop. A QR code next to the box lets customers check the online-only colourways at true size, without the store stocking every colour in every size.
