How Canadian Home Decor Stores Sell to Distant Shoppers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a home decor store owner in Toronto. She sells mirrors, wall art, lamps and vases from one shop and a website. Her local customers walk in, hold a vase, and buy with confidence. Her distant customers cannot do any of that. A shopper in Calgary likes a mirror online. A shopper in Halifax likes a lamp. Both stare at photos and wonder one thing. Will it look right in my room, and will it fit the space I have?

That question is the whole distant sale. Answer it, and the shopper buys. Leave it unanswered, and the shopper closes the tab. No discount fixes it. A better photo does not fix it either. The shopper does not need a prettier picture. The shopper needs to see the piece where it would actually stand.

The distance problem is a size problem

Decor is the hardest category to buy from far away. A sofa buyer can measure a wall and trust the number. A decor buyer rarely does. A mirror that looks generous online can swallow a hallway. A wall art print that looked bold on a white background can look lost above a sideboard. A vase that looked tall in the photo can miss the shelf by a few centimetres.

Local shoppers solve this by visiting. They stand in front of the piece. They hold it up to the light. Distant shoppers have none of that. They have a photo, a listed size, and a guess. Most of them guess wrong, or they refuse to guess at all. Both outcomes cost the store a sale.

This is why distance is not a shipping problem first. Shipping is solved. The sale dies before shipping ever matters, at the moment the shopper cannot picture the piece in their own room.

What the distant shopper actually checks

AR (Augmented Reality) answers that one question. The shopper points their phone at their own wall or shelf. The piece appears where it would really stand, at its true size. Too big is obvious. Too small is obvious. The colour sits in their own daylight, not studio light. The guessing ends.

That check takes seconds, and it needs no app. Each piece gets a link and a QR code that opens the AR (Augmented Reality) view in the phone browser. The shopper is already on their phone. The view opens where they already are. Try it yourself on our live decor demo. It opens in the browser, no install.

Trust is the sale

A distant shopper is not asking for a trick. They are asking for proof. A view that matches the real piece builds that proof. A view that is even slightly off destroys it. So the check that matters is simple. Does the view on the phone match the piece in the shop, at the same size?

That proof also changes what happens after the order. A shopper who saw the mirror at true size before buying has nothing left to discover when the box arrives. Fewer surprises mean fewer returns, and fewer returns mean the distant sale stays a sale.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, near or far.
