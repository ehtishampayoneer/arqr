How German Furniture Stores Sell to Small-Apartment Shoppers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a furniture store owner in Munich. She sells sofas, armchairs, dining sets, and beds, mostly to people who live in apartments under 70 square meters. Her best sellers leave the warehouse every week. Her most returned pieces come right back.

Small rooms punish bad guesses. In a large house, a sofa that is 20 centimeters too deep is a shrug. In a 55 square meter apartment, it blocks the balcony door. Shoppers measure twice and still get it wrong, because few people can picture 196 by 88 centimeters against their own living room wall.

The question every listing gets

Will this fit my room? That is the question hiding behind most bounces and most returns. Careful shoppers tape outlines on the floor. Most do not. They buy on hope and return on disappointment. The store that answers the size question on the product page, before the buy button, keeps the sale and skips the return.

What the AR view does

AR (Augmented Reality) puts the sofa in the shopper's own room at its true size. The customer opens the product page on their phone, taps the link, points the phone at the floor, and the sofa appears. They walk around it. They check whether the armchair clears the radiator. They try the two-seater, then the three-seater, and buy the one that fits. Try the live furniture demo on your own phone. It opens in the browser. No app to install.

This is a done-for-you service, not a software project. You send photos of your bestselling pieces and their real listed dimensions. Our designing department produces true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views from them and checks every one against the dimensions before it ships. A view that is even slightly off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted. Your first AR view is free, so you can check it against the real piece yourself.

One more number, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. For a store selling into small apartments, the effect runs both ways. Confident buyers buy, and buyers who saw the true size return less.

Where the links and QR codes go

  • Next to the size selector on every product page. That is where the doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button turns a sizing question into a two-minute answer.
  • On the price tags in the showroom. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. A customer looking at a two-seater in store can check the three-seater at home and come back decided.
  • In your chats and emails. Size questions arrive by message as much as they arrive by browsing. An AR link answers the question with the thing itself.

The follow-up that keeps it selling

New ranges arrive through the year. When yours does, you send its photos and our designing department adds the AR views to your catalogue. The follow-ups are part of the service, not a new project each time. Every piece on your site, live at true size, as fast as you can photograph it.
