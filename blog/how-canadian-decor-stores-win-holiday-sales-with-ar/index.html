How Canadian Decor Stores Win Holiday Sales with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Toronto in late November. Her shelves are full of lamps, mirrors, vases, and wall art. The holiday rush is here. Her website traffic is up, but so are the questions. Will this lamp fit my side table. Will this mirror suit my hallway. Is this vase too tall for the mantel. Shoppers want to buy, but they cannot answer those questions from a flat photo, so they wait, or they buy and send it back.

That is the holiday problem for decor stores. The season is short. Every returned gift costs shipping twice, ties up stock, and eats the margin on the sale. AR (Augmented Reality) views fix the exact question that causes the return: what will this look like, at its true size, in my room.

Why decor gifts come back

Most decor returns are not about quality. They are about size and fit in a real room. A lamp that looked right online crowds a small side table. A mirror that looked grand in a studio photo overwhelms a narrow hallway. A vase that looked modest online turns out tall for the shelf it was bought for. The shopper did nothing wrong. They guessed, because the store gave them nothing better than a photo to guess from.

An AR (Augmented Reality) view ends the guess. The shopper taps a link on the product page, points their phone at their own room, and the piece appears at its true size. They walk around it. They check it against the table, the wall, and the shelf. The decision moves from hope to sight, and that is what keeps the gift in the home after the holidays.

The holiday window is short, and most of it is after closing

Holiday browsing peaks in the evening, when the shop is closed and the website is open. A shopper on the couch at 10pm is deciding right then. If the size question stays unanswered, the tab closes and the sale waits for another day that may never come. If the shopper can check the true size at 10pm, the order can happen at 10pm.

This is where AR (Augmented Reality) earns its keep for a small store. It works while the shop is dark. It answers the size question for every visitor, not just the ones who walk in during opening hours. A store in one city can sell with the same confidence to a shopper in another province, because both shoppers see the piece in their own room.
