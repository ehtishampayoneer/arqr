How UK Rug Stores Cut Size Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Manchester. She sells Persian and modern rugs from one shop and a website. Her returns tell the same story every month. The rug comes back because of size. It looked right on the screen. It looked wrong on the floor.

This is the costliest return in the rug business. Shipping a rolled rug costs real money, twice. The rug comes back with footprints on it and sells used the second time. The fix is simple. Let the shopper see the rug at its true size on their own floor before they order.

Why rugs are the size problem

A rug is big, and the room around it changes how it looks. A 160 by 230 rug looks grand in a studio photo. On your own floor, next to your own sofa, it can look lost. Most buyers never tape out the size. They look at the photo, feel the size, and hope.

Picture a buyer in Leeds. She orders a runner for her hallway. The listing says 80 by 300. She reads the numbers. She still cannot picture 300 centimetres running down her hall. The runner arrives. It covers the door she needs open. She packs it up and returns it. Nobody lied to her. The listing had the size. She just could not picture it. That gap between the listed size and the pictured size is where most rug returns come from.

What true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views change

AR (Augmented Reality) lets the shopper point their phone at their own floor and see the rug there at its true size. No app. No measuring tape. She taps the link on the product page, scans the floor, and the rug appears in her hallway. She walks around it. She checks the door clears the edge. She orders knowing, not guessing.

Shopify reports that products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. That is Shopify's own data, not ours. The mechanism is plain. Shoppers who can see the true size hesitate less and return less.
