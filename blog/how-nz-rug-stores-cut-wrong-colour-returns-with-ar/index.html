How NZ Rug Stores Cut Wrong-Colour Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Auckland. She sells hand knotted rugs online to shoppers all over New Zealand. A rug comes back. The customer loved it on the screen. In her living room it looked the wrong colour, flat orange against her pale timber floor, nothing like the deep red on the product page. The store pays the return shipping, checks the rug, rolls it back up, and puts it back on the shelf. The sale is gone. The cost stays.

Returns like this are the quiet cost of selling rugs online in New Zealand. They rarely come from damage or a faulty weave. They come from colour. The shopper guessed, and the guess was wrong.

Why colour is the hardest thing to sell from a photo

A photo shows the rug in someone else's room, under someone else's light. The shopper's room is different. Her windows are big and the New Zealand sun comes in hard. Her floor is pale timber. Her walls are white, or grey, or deep green. In a bright room a deep red rug that glowed in the studio photo can read flat orange on her floor. A rug that looked warm beige on the screen can read cold grey next to her sofa.

The shopper knows this. She zooms in on the photo, reads the colour name, checks the dimensions, and still has to guess. Most of the time the guess is close enough and she keeps the rug. Often enough it is not, and the rug comes back. Each return costs the store shipping both ways, the time to check and re-roll, and days when the rug sits unsold. A few returns a month add up to real money, and a returned customer rarely buys again.

What changes when she checks it in her own room

AR (Augmented Reality) moves the check from after delivery to before buying. The shopper points her phone at her own floor and the rug appears there, at its true size, in the light of her own room, next to her own sofa. She checks the runner in the hallway. She holds the big rug under the coffee table she already owns. The check takes ten seconds, and the colour surprise is gone before checkout.

That is the whole mechanism. A photo asks the shopper to imagine the colour in her room. An AR (Augmented Reality) view asks her to look at it there. When she can see the rug where it will actually live, the question changes from will the colour work to when can I have it.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size and the true colour hesitate less, and shoppers who hesitate less buy more and return less.

Where the colour check belongs

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where the buying decision happens. A see it in your room link beside the buy button answers the colour question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same rug in AR. Customers check it at home against their own floors and come back decided.
  • In your social posts. A short clip of a shopper checking a rug in her own room shows the idea faster than any paragraph. The viewer sees the check happen and wants the same certainty.
