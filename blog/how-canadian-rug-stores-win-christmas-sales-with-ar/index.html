How Canadian Rug Stores Win Christmas Sales with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Toronto. She sells wool rugs, runners and flatweaves from one shop and a website. November arrives and the Christmas shoppers arrive with it. They browse her product pages in the evening, find a rug they like, and stop. They cannot tell if it will suit the room it is meant for. The room is not theirs. It belongs to the person getting the gift, or it is their own living room and they still cannot picture the size.

That shopper is not saying no. She is saying she cannot tell yet. At Christmas the doubt is doubled. A rug is a big gift and a big piece. It has to fit the room, sit right under the table, and look right with the furniture already there. Some shoppers buy anyway and guess wrong. Those rugs come back in January. The rest never buy at all. Both cost the store, and both are avoidable.

Why Christmas rugs are hard to buy from a photo

A photo of a rug on a white background gives the shopper nothing to measure against. No sofa, no table, no doorway. A 160 by 230 rug and a 200 by 290 rug can look the same in a square photo. Pattern scale is worse. A pattern that looks bold on screen can look busy across a whole living room floor, or fade away entirely.

Gift buying makes it worse. The giver is guessing twice, once about taste and once about size. She has no room to measure, no floor to stand on, and a delivery date she cannot miss. So she picks the safe gift instead, or she buys the rug and plans to return it if it does not fit. Either way, the store loses a sale it could have won, or pays for a return it could have avoided.

What the shopper actually checks

AR (Augmented Reality) answers one question. How does this rug look in my room, at its true size. The shopper points their phone at their floor. The rug appears where it would really lie. Too big is obvious. Too small is obvious. The pattern against the real sofa and the real light is obvious. The guessing ends.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less and return less. For a Christmas gift, that confidence is the difference between a sale in November and a return in January.
