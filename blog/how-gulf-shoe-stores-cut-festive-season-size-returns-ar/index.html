How Gulf Shoe Stores Cut Festive-Season Size Returns With True-Size AR (Augmented Reality) Try-On

Picture a shoe store owner in Dubai. She sells heels, boots, and sneakers from a shop in the mall and a website that ships across the Gulf. November is when the season turns. National Day sales start, then the Christmas gifting rush, then the party season. Orders spike. And a few weeks later, so do the returns.

Not because the shoes were wrong. Because the sizes were guesses.

This is the honest version.

Why the festive shoe order is a size guess

Festive shoes are bought fast, and often by someone who never tries them on. A man orders a pair of dress shoes for his wife the week before a National Day dinner. A woman in Riyadh buys party heels at midnight from a store in Dubai. A mother sends sneakers to her son studying in Sharjah.

None of them tries the shoe on. They read the size chart, pick the number they think is right, and hope. Shoe sizes run differently between brands and between countries. A UK 6 is not the same as a US 6, and half the buyers in the Gulf shop from UK brands, US brands, and local brands in the same week. The size chart helps. It does not answer the real question: what does this shoe look like, at its true size, on my own foot.

A wrong-size shoe after a festive order is not a small problem. The party date passes, so the customer does not wait for an exchange. They send it back and buy nothing. And in the Gulf the return often crosses a border, which makes the freight cost real.

What true-size AR (Augmented Reality) try-on changes

The customer taps the link on your product page. Their phone opens the AR (Augmented Reality) view in the browser, no app to install. They point the camera at their own foot and the shoe appears on it at its true size, checked against the real listed dimensions.

They see the shape on their own foot. They compare the length and width against their own feet. They turn the foot and look from the side. Two minutes, no guessing. A shoe bought with that check is a shoe that stays.

Gift buyers get the same check. The man buying the heels for his wife opens the try-on from his couch and confirms the size against a shoe she already owns, placed beside it on screen. And smart stores now tuck a small card with a QR code in the gift box. The recipient opens it on their own phone and confirms the true size at home before the receipt is even out of the box. Where the links and QR codes go

  • Next to the size guide on every product page. That is where the doubt lives. A try-it-on-your-own-foot link beside the size chart turns the number guess into a two minute check.
  • In the gift box. A small card with a QR code costs almost nothing and stops the gift-box exchange before it starts.
  • In your chat threads. A lot of Gulf shoe buying happens through Instagram DMs and WhatsApp. An AR (Augmented Reality) link shared in the thread answers the size question without a store visit.
  • On the in-store tag. A QR code on the shelf tag lets the browsing customer check the rest of your range on their own feet without waiting for their size to be found.
