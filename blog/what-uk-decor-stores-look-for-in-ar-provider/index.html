What UK Home Decor Stores Look for in an AR (Augmented Reality) Provider

Picture a home decor store owner in Bristol. She sells vases, lamps, mirrors, and wall art from one shop and a website. She is ready to put AR (Augmented Reality) on her product pages, and three providers have sent her quotes. The quotes do not look like they describe the same product. Here is the checklist that cuts through them.

It comes down to four questions. Who produces the views. Whether the shopper needs an app. How true size is proven. What the real price is for one year. Ask all four in writing before you sign anything.

1. Who produces the views, and from what

Start here, because everything else follows. Ask the provider a plain question: who makes the AR views of my lamps and mirrors, and what do they start from?

On some routes, you supply the files yourself. The provider sells a viewer, you commission the files from artists, and you manage the whole pipeline. For a 60 piece catalogue, that bill is the project, not the software.

On the done-for-you route, you send photos and your real listed dimensions, and the provider produces the true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views. ARQR360 works this way. Our designing department produces the views for you, from your photos, with new season stock handled the same way. When you compare quotes, compare the full job: views plus viewer plus hosting, not the monthly fee alone.

2. Does the shopper need an app

Ask before you sign: does the customer need to download an app to open the view? If the answer is yes, most of them will not use it. People do not install apps to check a vase. A view that opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code, gets used. Test that on your own phone with our live decor demo.

3. How is true size proven

For decor, size is the whole question. A mirror that is even a little off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted. Ask the provider how they check every view against the real listed dimensions, and what happens when a piece is off. A serious answer names a process. A vague answer is the answer.

Ask too what the shopper actually sees. The honest promise for decor is size and look in the room: the lamp at its true size beside the sofa, the mirror at its true size above the console table, so the shopper can check it against the room they already live in. That is what ends the size guessing that stops so many orders.

4. What does one year actually cost

Get the quote in writing for your full catalogue, for one year, with everything included. The monthly fee is usually the smallest line. The rest is per-piece production fees, per-view charges that grow with your traffic, setup fees, and the cost of new season stock. A quote that will not put that number in writing is a quote you cannot compare.

Where the links go

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your shoppers browse.
