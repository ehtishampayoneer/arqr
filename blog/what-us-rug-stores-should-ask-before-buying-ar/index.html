What US Rug Stores Should Ask Before Buying AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Dallas. She sells Persian rugs, modern wool rugs, and runners, from one shop and a website that ships across the country. Her customers keep asking the same question before they buy: will this rug fit my room? She has decided AR is the answer. Now she is shopping for a provider, and every website promises the same thing.

They do not deliver the same thing. Some providers sell software and leave the store to build everything itself. Some build the whole thing for the store and hand over links that work the day they arrive. The difference is worth real money, and it shows up in the questions you ask before you sign anything. Here are the six that matter most for a rug store.

1. Who builds the AR views, and from what?

This is the question that decides the whole project. Some providers hand you a viewer and expect you to supply the finished files for every rug. If you have no files and no designer on staff, the project stops before it starts, or you pay someone else to build the files first.

Ask it plainly: do you build the views for me, and what do you need from me to start? With ARQR360 the answer is simple. You send photos of your rugs and their real listed dimensions, bestsellers first, and our designing department produces the true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views. No files to supply. No designer to hire. No new skill to learn.

2. What is the full first year cost?

A monthly fee is only one line of the bill. Ask for the whole first year in writing: the setup fee, the monthly fee, any charge per view or per product, and what new rugs cost when the season changes. A low monthly fee with a per-product charge turns expensive the moment your catalogue grows.

ARQR360 publishes its price: $249 one-time setup and $29 a month, with all AR (Augmented Reality) view production included. No product cap, no per-view fees. Your first test view is free, so you can check the quality before spending anything.

3. How do you prove the rug is true to size?

A rug view that is even a few inches off teaches the shopper the tool cannot be trusted. She checks the rug against her sofa, sees it does not match, and never uses the link again. Worse, she may buy on a wrong size and send it back.

Ask every provider how they check each view against the real rug. The honest answer names the real listed dimensions and a check against them before the view goes live. Ask to see the check on your own bestselling rug before you commit to the whole catalogue. That is exactly what the free test view is for.

4. Does my shopper need an app?

If the answer is yes, most of your shoppers will never use it. People do not install an app to look at a rug. A link or a QR code that opens in the phone browser gets used. An app download gets skipped, and the sale goes back to guessing from photos.

With ARQR360 there is no app. Each rug gets a link and a QR code that opens the AR (Augmented Reality) view in the phone browser. Try the live rug demo on your own phone and point it at your floor.

5. How fast do my rugs go live, and what do I have to do?

Ask for a written timeline for your catalogue size, and ask what the provider needs from you at each step. A provider who needs your files, your logins, and three rounds of integration work is selling you a project, not a service.

With ARQR360 the project is: send photos, get a working AR catalogue back in about seven days. The links go on your product pages. The QR codes go in the shop. Our team follows up after launch to make sure the views are working where shoppers actually see them, and custom packages are available if your store needs something beyond the standard catalogue.

6. What happens when I add new rugs, or if I leave?

Rug stores change stock every season. Ask what a new rug costs to add, how long it takes, and whether there is a limit. Then ask what happens to your views if you end the contract. Get both answers in writing before you sign. A provider who cannot answer plainly is telling you something.
