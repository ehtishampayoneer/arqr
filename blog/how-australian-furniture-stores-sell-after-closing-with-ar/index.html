How Australian Furniture Stores Sell After Closing Time with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a furniture store owner in Melbourne. Her shop closes at 5.30pm. Her website stays open all night. She knows the pattern by now. The orders that come in during the day are the easy ones. The browsers who arrive after dinner are the ones who leave without buying.

They are not leaving because they dislike the furniture. They leave because they cannot answer one question from a photo. Will this sofa fit my living room, and will it look right in my light? At 9pm there is nobody in the shop to answer them. So they close the tab and promise themselves they will come back. Most of them never do.

The sale that used to wait till morning

Picture a couple in Brisbane. They have spent the weekend looking at sofas. On Sunday night they sit down with a laptop, find a three seater they like, and stare at the listed size. 220 centimetres wide. They know their wall is about three metres. They do not know if the sofa will crowd the doorway, or if the colour will fight with their rug.

Nobody is lying to them. The listing has the size. But a number on a page is not a picture in a room. That gap between the listed size and the real room is where evening sales die. The shopper does not want a better photo. The shopper wants to see the piece where it would actually stand.

What the evening shopper actually checks

AR (Augmented Reality) answers that one question. The shopper points their phone at their own floor. The piece appears where it would really stand, at its true size. Too wide is obvious. Too deep is obvious. The colour sits in their own evening light, not studio light. The guessing ends.

That check takes seconds, and it needs no app. Each piece gets a link and a QR code that opens the AR (Augmented Reality) view in the phone browser. The shopper is already on their phone. The view opens where they already are. Try it yourself on our live furniture demo. It opens in the browser, no install.

Why after hours is where AR earns its keep

During the day, a shopper can drive to the shop, sit on the sofa, and ask a staff member. After hours, the website has to do all of that alone. A photo cannot. A size chart cannot. A true to size view in the shopper's own room can, because it replaces the visit the shopper cannot make.

It also changes the next morning. A shopper who checked the sofa at night comes back decided. The call to the shop is shorter. The questions are better. The visit, if they still want one, is to buy, not to wonder. Stores that sell after hours do not just gain night orders. They gain faster daytime sales too.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, at any hour.
