How US Decor Stores Sell After Closing with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Austin. She sells wall art, mirrors, vases, lamps, and table pieces from one shop and a website. Her shop closes at 6pm. Her website never closes, but after 6pm it barely sells. Her customers browse on the sofa at 10pm, fall in love with a piece, and then they stop. They cannot tell if it will suit their wall or fit their table, and there is no one in the shop to ask.

That evening browser is most of the growth in US decor sales. She works all day, she shops at night, and she buys when she feels sure. A photo cannot make her sure. A true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page can, and it works at 10pm just as well as it works at 10am. Here is how a US decor store keeps selling after closing, without staying open a minute longer.

Why evening shoppers leave without buying

A decor piece is a judgement call, and at night the buyer is making it alone. The questions in her head are simple, and a photo cannot answer any of them.

First, will it fit the space. A mirror that looks right in your bright shop can be too wide for her hallway, or too small above her sideboard and look lost. A vase that looks generous in a photo can vanish on a big table. Second, will the colour work in her light. A piece that glows warm under your shop lights can look cold in her living room, or clash with the wall she already has. Third, will it suit the room. A bold wall piece looks great in a photo and can take over a small room in real life, and a quiet piece can vanish against a dark wall.

She does not want to get it wrong. A decor piece is not a small thing to send back. It is fragile, it costs real money to ship, and the return feels like a failure. So she browses, she hesitates, and she buys nothing. Or she bookmarks the page and forgets it by morning.

What changes when she sees it at true size

A true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view answers all three questions in about a minute, on her own phone, in her own room, at any hour. She taps the link on the product page, points her phone at her wall, and the piece appears at its true size, right where it would hang. She steps back. She checks it against her sofa. She sees the colour in her own light, next to her own furniture.

Our designing department builds each view from the real listed dimensions of the piece, so the size on screen is the size that arrives at the door. That is what turns a nervous evening browser into a confident buyer. She stops asking if it will suit the room and starts asking when it will arrive. One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and evening shoppers hesitate most of all, because there is no one in the shop to reassure them.
