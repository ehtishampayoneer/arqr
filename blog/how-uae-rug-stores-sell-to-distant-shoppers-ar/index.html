How UAE Rug Stores Sell to Distant Shoppers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Dubai. Her shop sits on a busy street, but half her orders come from people who never walk in. They live in Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ras Al Khaimah, or further afield in Doha and Riyadh. They find a rug on her website, they like the pattern, and then they stop. The question holding them back is simple. Will it fit my room, and will the colour match what I already have?

A photo cannot answer that. A distant shopper cannot lay the rug on their floor, step back, and look. So they ask for more photos, they ask for the exact size again, and often they go quiet. This article is about the fix that closes that gap: a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view of the rug, on the shopper's own floor, opened from a link.

The distance problem is a size problem

For a rug, distance is not about shipping. Couriers carry rugs across the Emirates and the Gulf every day. Distance is about trust. A shopper who cannot visit the shop has to trust a flat photo for a piece that will cover a large part of their living room. If the size is wrong, the return costs time and money for both sides, and the shopper knows it before they order.

That is why distant shoppers hesitate longer than local ones. They are not unsure about the rug. They are unsure about the fit. Give them a way to check the fit in their own room, and the distance stops mattering.

What the AR view does on the product page

Every rug in the catalogue gets a link and a QR code. The shopper taps the link on their phone, points the phone at the floor, and the rug appears in their own room at its true size. They walk around it. They check it against the sofa and the doorway. They see whether the pattern sits right with the furniture they already own.

Nothing needs to be installed. The view opens in the phone browser, from the product page or from a QR code on the tag. That matters for a distant shopper, because asking them to install an app before they have decided is asking them to leave.

How a store uses it to sell further away

Start with the rugs that already sell. Put the view link next to the price on every product page, where the buying decision happens. A link buried in a menu does nothing. Put the QR code on the swing tag and on the packaging, so a shopper who saw the rug in your shop can check it at home and order the right size.

Then use the links where distant shoppers actually ask questions. A shopper messages on WhatsApp from another city and asks if a rug will fit. Send the link, not another photo. They open it, place the rug on their floor, and answer their own question. Our team follows up after launch to make sure the views are working on the pages where shoppers actually see them, and new season rugs get the same treatment as the first batch. What to put on the page so a faraway shopper trusts it

Size first, always. The listed dimensions should sit next to the view link, so the shopper can check the number against what they see on their floor. Then colour in real light. A rug photographed under shop lights can look different at home, and the AR view shows it in the light of the shopper's own room.

Be honest about what the view does. It shows size and look: the rug at its true size, on the shopper's own floor, next to their own furniture. It cannot tell a shopper how the pile feels underfoot, and no honest seller promises that. What it can do is end the size guessing that stops so many distant orders.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less.
