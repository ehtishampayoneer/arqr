How European Rug Stores Sell to Distant Shoppers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Lyon. She sells wool rugs, runners, and flatweaves from one shop and a website. Her best customers live nowhere near Lyon. They are in Berlin, in Amsterdam, in Milan. They found her rugs online, they love the pattern, and then they stop. They cannot walk into her shop and see the rug on a floor. They cannot hold it up against their sofa. So they keep browsing, and they buy from the shop that makes them feel sure.

Distance is the hardest problem in rug sales. A rug is a big purchase, and it has to fit the room it is meant for. A shopper who lives far away has no way to check that from a photo. Here is how a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page closes that distance, and turns a faraway browser into a buyer.

Why distant shoppers are the hardest sale

A local shopper can come in, unroll the rug, and see it on your floor. A distant shopper has none of that. Her questions are simple, and a photo cannot answer any of them.

First, will it fit. A 160 by 230 rug and a 200 by 290 rug can look the same in a square photo, and on her floor they are very different things. One sits right under the coffee table. The other covers half the room. Second, will the colour work in her light. A rug that glows warm in your shop can look flat in her living room, or clash with the sofa she already owns. Third, will the pattern suit the room. A bold pattern looks great on screen and can take over a small room in real life, while a quiet pattern can vanish against a dark floor.

She does not want to get it wrong. A rug that comes back from another country costs her the courier both ways, and it costs you the sale plus a rug that needs checking before it can sell again. So she browses, she hesitates, and she buys nothing. Or she buys from a big online shop, even if it is not the rug she wanted, because the big shop feels safer.

What changes when the rug appears on her own floor

A true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view answers all three questions in about a minute, on her own phone, in her own room. She taps the link on the product page, points her phone at her floor, and the rug appears at its true size, right where it would lie. She steps back. She checks it against her sofa and her coffee table. She sees the colour in her own light, next to her own furniture.

Our designing department builds each view from the real listed dimensions of the rug, so the size on screen is the size that arrives at the door. That is what turns a nervous distant shopper into a confident one. She stops asking if it will fit and starts asking when it will arrive. One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and distant shoppers hesitate most of all, because they cannot visit your shop.

Gift buyers are distant shoppers too

Many of your faraway orders are gifts. The buyer is in one city and the rug is meant for a home in another. She cannot walk into the recipient's living room with a tape measure. She is guessing twice: the taste and the size. A link she can send to the person she is buying for changes that. The recipient opens it on their own phone, points it at their own floor, and checks the rug at true size in the room it will live in. The guesswork is gone before the order is placed, and the January return never happens.
