ARQR360 vs WEARFITS: What a Shoe Store Actually Gets from Each

Picture a shoe store owner in Manchester. He sells handmade leather boots online and from one small shop. Shoppers love the look. They open the size guide, compare it against their own shoes, and still hesitate. Nobody wants to guess with two hundred pound boots. So he looks into virtual try-on, and one name keeps coming up: WEARFITS. Then a retailer friend mentions ARQR360. Two options, two websites, no clear answer on which one fits a store like his.

This is the honest version.

What WEARFITS is

WEARFITS is a virtual try-on platform built around footwear and bags, the categories where tracking a real foot accurately is the hard part. Retailers add it through its API, SDK, or plugins for Shopify and WooCommerce. The platform includes AI-assisted 3D generation from 2D imagery, which is its answer to the biggest bottleneck in shoe AR: turning catalogue photos into 3D assets fast enough to cover a whole range.

It is a platform, which means your store runs the integration and manages the catalogue. On Shopify it advertises an entry point from $49 a month. That is the entry tier. What a real catalogue costs to run depends on your asset volume, your channels, and the integration setup, so treat the full figure as quote-based and ask for it in writing before you compare.

What ARQR360 is

ARQR360 is a done-for-you AR (Augmented Reality) catalogue for independent shoe stores. You send photos of your bestselling shoes and their real listed dimensions. We turn them into true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views, host them, and hand you links and QR codes that open the view in your customer's phone browser. No app. No platform to learn. No 3D team on your side.

The audience is different. ARQR360 is built for stores that sell shoes off the shelf, their own range or other brands, not retailers running platform integrations with in-house developers.

Price: entry tier vs one flat public rate

WEARFITS advertises from $49 a month on Shopify. That opens the platform door. The full annual cost of covering your catalogue depends on how many products you publish, which channels you sell on, and what the integration takes, so the real number is custom. Ask for a written quote for your exact catalogue size before you compare anything.

Setup effort: integrating a platform vs sending photos

A WEARFITS project is a platform integration. You pick the integration route, connect your catalogue, and manage the pipeline as new ranges arrive. Its AI-assisted 3D generation shortens the asset work, which is genuinely useful for a store with a wide range. It is still a project your side has to run.

With ARQR360 the project is: send photos, get a working AR (Augmented Reality) catalogue back in about seven days. The links go on your product pages. The QR codes go in the shop. There is nothing to integrate and nothing to maintain.

True-to-size accuracy, and an honest word on shoes

Both routes show your shoes at true size. That matters, because a boot even slightly off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted. Ask both of us how scale is verified against the real shoe before you sign.

One honest line about footwear specifically. AR can show the true size of a shoe and let a shopper compare it against their own feet, but it cannot tell them how the shoe feels to wear. No platform should promise that. The honest promise is a real size idea on their own feet, in their own room, before they buy.

Who builds the 3D assets

With WEARFITS, the platform includes AI-assisted 3D generation from your 2D imagery, and you run the integration and the catalogue. You manage the source photos, the platform handles the asset production side.

With ARQR360, there is no pipeline for you to run at all. Your photos go in, finished true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views come back, hosted and ready. Your first one is free so you can check it against the real shoe before spending anything.
