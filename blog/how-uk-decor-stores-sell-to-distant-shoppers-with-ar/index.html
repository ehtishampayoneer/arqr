How UK Decor Stores Sell to Distant Shoppers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Bath. She sells framed prints, mirrors, table lamps, and vases from one shop and a website. Her best customers in the shop buy on the spot. They hold a frame up to the light, set a lamp on a table, and see it work. Her online customers are a different story. They live in Manchester, in Glasgow, in London. They will never walk into her shop. They browse her website, love a piece, and then they stop. The piece is right, but the room is far away, and they cannot tell if it will suit their home.

That is the distant shopper problem, and for a UK decor store it is most of the market. Your website reaches the whole country. Your shop reaches one street. Here is how a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page closes that gap, so a shopper three hundred miles away buys with the same confidence as the customer standing in your shop.

Why distant shoppers hesitate

A decor piece is bought for one specific place in one specific room. A mirror goes over one mantel. A lamp sits on one bedside table. A framed print hangs on one wall. The shopper far away cannot try any of that. She looks at your photo and asks three questions she cannot answer from the screen.

First, will it fit. A 90cm mirror sounds fine until it hangs over a 100cm mantel and looks lost, or over an 80cm mantel and hangs over the edges. Second, will the colour work in her light. A print that glows in your bright shop window can look flat in her north facing living room. Third, will it suit the things around it. A vase that looks right alone can look wrong next to her sofa, and a lamp that looks plain in a photo can look right once it sits on her table.

Photos cannot answer any of that. Dimensions on a listing cannot answer it either. Most shoppers cannot picture 90cm on a wall from numbers on a screen. So they browse, they hesitate, and they buy nothing, or they buy from the store that answers the question first.

What the shopper sees changes everything

A true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view answers all three questions in about a minute, on the shopper's own phone, in her own room. She taps the link on the product page, points her phone at her wall, and the mirror appears at its true size, right where it would hang. She steps back. She checks it against her mantel. She sees the colour in her own light, next to her own sofa.

Our designing department builds each view from the real listed dimensions of the piece, so the size on screen is the size that arrives at the door. That is what turns a distant browser into a buyer. She stops asking if it will fit and starts asking when it will arrive. One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and distant shoppers hesitate most of all, because they cannot visit your shop to check.

Where the view goes on your site

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where the size doubt lives. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters, for the shopper in Glasgow just as much as the one in Bath.
  • On the price tags and packaging in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. No programming, no changes to your website needed. We send you a QR code for each product. Put it on the product page, on your packaging, in your shop, on social media, anywhere. Your buyer scans it and sees your product in their own room, real size. A customer who likes a piece in the shop can check it at home that evening and order it from her sofa.
  • In your posts and emails. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your distant shoppers browse. A short post with the link in it reaches buyers at the hour they actually shop, wherever they live.
