ARQR360 vs Zakeke: What a Rug Store Actually Gets from Each

Picture a rug store owner in Austin. She sells handmade wool rugs online and from one small showroom. A single 8x10 can cost the buyer two thousand dollars, and returns eat her margin alive. Shoppers ask the same question every time: will it fit my room. So she starts looking at AR. One name keeps coming up: Zakeke. Then a retailer friend mentions ARQR360. Two options, two websites, no clear answer on which one fits a store like hers.

This is the honest version.

What Zakeke is

Zakeke is a visual commerce platform from Italy, founded in 2017. It gives online stores a 3D configurator, an AR viewer, virtual try-on, and a 3D digital asset manager, all in one cloud product. It plugs into Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Magento, Etsy, and others. Customers can spin products around, change options, and view items in their own space through a link or QR code. It is a real, established product with over 10,000 customers across hundreds of sectors.

It is a platform, which means your store runs it. You sign up, connect it to your store, and upload your own 3D model for every product. Their own setup guides walk you through uploading one 3D model per product. If you do not have 3D files, you hire a 3D artist or a generation service. Some merchants run it well. But if you are a small rug shop with no 3D person on staff, that is your first project, not a detail.

Pricing is a monthly subscription with tiered plans. Software review sites list plans in the tens of dollars per month, with a small transaction fee on customized orders. Get the current price list from their site before you commit, because tiers and limits change.

What ARQR360 is

ARQR360 is a done-for-you AR (Augmented Reality) catalogue for independent rug stores. You send photos of your bestselling rugs and their real listed dimensions. We turn them into true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views, host them, and hand you links and QR codes. No app, no integration, no changes to your website. Your buyer scans the code and sees the rug in their own room, real size.

The four things that actually matter

Price. Zakeke charges a monthly subscription, tiered by plan, plus a small fee on customized orders. You also pay whatever it costs to get 3D models made for your catalogue, whether that is a freelancer or an in-house hire. ARQR360 is flat and public: $249 one-time setup and $29 a month, with all view creation included. The first test view is free.

Setup effort. With Zakeke, your store does the integration, the product setup, and the ongoing catalogue management. It is straightforward software, but it is a project. With ARQR360 there is no project. You send photos, we build the views, you paste the QR codes on your product pages. A store can go live with its bestsellers in days.

True-to-size accuracy. Zakeke's AR viewer shows your 3D model at scale in the customer's room. The accuracy is only as good as the model you uploaded. Get the dimensions wrong in the file and the rug renders wrong. With ARQR360, true-to-size is the whole product: every view is calibrated to your rug's real listed size before it goes live.

Who does the modelling. With Zakeke, you do. You bring the 3D files or pay someone to make them. With ARQR360, we do. You bring photos and measurements. Claim your free AR product and check our work against the real rug before you spend anything.

Which store should pick which

If you sell customizable products and you already have 3D assets or a designer on retainer, Zakeke is a serious platform and worth a demo. If you are a rug store with no 3D person and no interest in becoming a software project manager, the done-for-you route gets you true-to-size AR in your customers' rooms this month instead of next quarter.
