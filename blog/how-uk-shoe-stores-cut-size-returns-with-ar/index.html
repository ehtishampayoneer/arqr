How UK Shoe Stores Cut Size Returns with AR (No App Needed)

Picture a shoe store owner in Leeds. She sells leather boots and trainers from one shop and a website. Every Monday, the post brings back boxes. Boots that did not fit. Trainers a half size too small. Each return costs her the postage, the inspection time, and a pair that comes back scuffed and no longer sellable at full price.

She is not alone, and the cause is not her shoes. It is the size guess. Online, a customer picks a size from a chart, hopes the brand runs true, and waits. When it does not fit, the store pays.

Why size charts do not fix it

UK shoe sizes add their own confusion. A size 7 in one brand fits like a 6 in another. A wide foot in a standard last feels a half size down. Size charts tell the customer the numbers are right, but they cannot tell her how the shoe sits on her own foot. So she orders two sizes and sends one back, or she orders one and sends it back.

AR try-on answers the actual question. The customer points her phone at her feet and sees the shoe on her own foot, at true size, in the light of her own room. She checks the shape, the length, the look, before the buy button. The guess goes away.

What AR try-on changes for the store

Fewer size mistakes mean fewer returns. It is that direct. The customer who sees the fit before buying is the customer who keeps the box. Shopify reports that products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. That is Shopify's own data, not ours, and it lines up with the mechanism: shoppers who can check the fit hesitate less and send fewer pairs back.

It also changes who buys. Customers who never order shoes online because of the fit gamble start ordering. That is new revenue, not just saved postage.

The app question

Ask every provider this before you sign: does the customer need to download an app? If the answer is yes, most of your customers will never use it. People do not install apps to look at a boot. Browser-based try-on opens from a link or a QR code in seconds. That is the version that actually gets used. Try it yourself first: our live footwear demo opens in your phone browser, no install.

What to check before you choose

  • True-to-size proof: for shoes, a view even slightly off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted. Ask how the provider verifies scale against the real shoe.
  • Who builds the models: on most routes the modelling bill, not the software, is the biggest cost. Ask what is included and what new-season stock costs.
  • Total cost: add the models, the setup, and the next range. Per-model fees quietly dwarf subscriptions.
  • Where the link lives: try-on buried in a menu nobody opens does nothing. The link belongs next to the price, where the buying decision happens.
