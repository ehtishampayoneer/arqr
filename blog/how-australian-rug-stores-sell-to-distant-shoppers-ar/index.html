How Australian Rug Stores Sell to Distant Shoppers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Melbourne. Her best rugs sell well in the shop. People walk in, stand on the rug, check the pile with their hand, and buy. Online is a different story. A shopper in Perth, or in a small town in Queensland, or across the water in Tasmania, sees the same rug on a screen and hesitates. She cannot stand on it. She cannot see it in her own room. So she closes the tab, or she buys and sends it back.

Distance is the problem. Not the rug. Not the price. The shopper is too far away to do the one thing that sells a rug: see it in the room where it will live.

Why distant shoppers hesitate

A rug is a big purchase and a big object. The wrong size ruins the room. A rug that is too small looks lost under the sofa. A rug that is too big crowds the chairs. Photos cannot settle it. A shopper in Brisbane looking at a photo of a rug in a Melbourne showroom has no idea how that rug will look on her own timber floor, in her own light, next to her own sofa.

Colour is the second worry. A rug that looks warm cream in a studio photo can look grey in a north facing room. A deep red can look brown in the evening light. The distant shopper knows this. She has been caught before. So she waits, and waiting usually means no sale.

When she does buy, the risk lands on the store. An interstate return is not a quick trip to the post office. It is freight, packing, inspection, and a rug that has been unrolled and rolled again. One distant return can eat the profit on several good orders.

What changes when she can see it at home

AR (Augmented Reality) puts the rug in her room before she buys. She taps a link on the product page, or scans a QR code, points her phone at her own floor, and the rug appears at its true size. She can walk around it. She can check the edge against the sofa leg. She can see the colour in her own light, at midday and in the evening.

That is the whole sale. The question she could not answer from Perth is answered on her own floor. She buys with the same confidence as the shopper who stood on the rug in the Melbourne showroom.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and shoppers who hesitate less buy more and return less.

How a store sets it up

It starts with photos. You send product photos and your listed dimensions, bestsellers first. Our designing department produces the true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views for you. Photos in, views out. Each rug gets a link and a QR code. About seven days from photos to a live catalogue.

Put the link where the distant shopper will find it. Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. In the order confirmation email, so the shopper can check once more before the rug ships. On the price tags in the shop, so a local shopper can check it at home and come back decided. In Instagram posts and direct messages, because that is where your distant shoppers browse.

No app is needed. The view opens in the phone browser. A shopper in Darwin or Hobart does not have to download anything, sign up for anything, or wait for anything. She scans, she sees, she decides.

What it does for the store

First, fewer interstate returns. Most distant returns are size returns, and size is exactly what the shopper can now check before the order. Second, more sales from places you cannot reach with a showroom. A shop in one city can sell with confidence to the whole country, because every shopper gets the same in-room check. Third, your team spends less time answering the same size questions by email and phone, because the shopper answers them herself, on her own floor.
