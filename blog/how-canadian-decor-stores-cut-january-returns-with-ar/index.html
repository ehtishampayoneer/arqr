How Canadian Decor Stores Cut January Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Toronto. Christmas is over. The shop is quiet again, and the returns start. A table lamp that was a gift, back in its box. A framed print bought in the Boxing Day sale, back because it looked too big on the wall. A vase that looked soft sage on the screen and reads mint green in the buyer's living room. Every one of those pieces has to be checked, repacked, and sold again, and some of them never sell at full price after that.

January is the hardest month for a decor store. December brings the sales. January brings the boxes back.

Why January returns hurt a decor store

A decor return is not like returning a shirt. The piece is fragile, or heavy, or both. A lamp has a shade that dents. A mirror has glass that chips. A framed print has corners that scuff. Every return costs the store the courier both ways, the time to inspect and repack, and a piece that may only sell again at a discount.

Gifts make it worse. The person who bought the lamp never saw the room it was going into. The person who received it did not choose it. When it does not suit the room, it comes back, and the store pays for a guess someone else made.

Sale pieces make it worse too. A shopper who buys in the Boxing Day rush is buying fast, on a screen, in a week when every store is shouting. She likes the lamp, she likes the price, and she guesses on the size. The guess is wrong more often than the store would like, and January is when the wrong guesses arrive at the counter.

What changes when the shopper checks first

AR (Augmented Reality) lets the shopper see the piece in her own room before she buys. She taps a link on the product page, or scans a QR code, points her phone at her own wall or table, and the piece appears at its true size. She can check the lamp against her side table. She can see the framed print on her own wall, in her own light, next to her own sofa. She can see the colour as it really looks in her room, not as it looked in a studio photo.

That is the whole fix. The question that used to be answered by a guess is answered on the shopper's own wall. The gift buyer can check the piece in the room it is going to, if she has a photo of that room, or send the link to the person receiving it so they can check it themselves. The Boxing Day shopper can check the size in ten seconds instead of guessing in the rush.

Where the links go

The view only helps if the shopper sees it before she buys. Put the link next to the price on every product page. That is where the size doubt lives, and that is where the buying decision happens. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.

Put a QR code on the price tag in the shop too. A customer browsing in the store can scan it, check the piece at home, and come back decided. And put the link in your January emails. A shopper who returned a piece in January is a shopper who still wants the right piece. Show her the link and she can check the replacement before she orders it.
