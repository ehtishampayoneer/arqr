How New Zealand Rug Stores Turn Browsers into Buyers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Auckland. She sells handmade rugs from one shop and a website that ships across both islands. Her product pages get steady traffic. Visitors zoom in on the photos, read the size in the description, then close the tab. The rugs sit unsold.

Most of those visitors are not put off by the price. They are put off by a question the photos cannot answer. Will this rug suit my room? Will it fit beside my sofa, or will it crowd the room? Will the colour work with my floor? Until she can answer that question, the shopper will not buy, and no discount will change her mind.

This is the gap between browsing and buying for a rug store. The shopper likes the rug. She just cannot see it in her own room. An AR (Augmented Reality) view closes that gap. She points her phone at her own floor, and the rug appears there at its true size, in the light of her own room. The doubt goes, and the order follows.

Why rug shoppers hesitate most

Rugs are the hardest thing to buy from a photo. The size is listed in centimetres, but almost nobody measures. The buyer looks at a photo, feels the size, and hopes. A rug that looks right on a screen can swallow a room or get lost in it. Colour is the same problem. A rug photographed in studio light can look warmer or cooler than it does on the floor at home, and New Zealand light is strong and honest.

Distance makes it harder still. A shopper in Christchurch or Dunedin cannot drive to your Auckland shop to stand on the rug. She buys from the website or she does not buy at all. If the website cannot show her the rug in her own room, she has no way to be sure, and the safe choice is to close the tab and keep looking.

That is why the browsers pile up and the buyers stay few. The store is not short of visitors. It is short of a way to let a visitor check the rug where it will actually live.

What changes when the shopper checks first

Put a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page, next to the price, and three things change.

First, the shopper stops guessing. She can see if the rug suits the sofa or crowds it. She can see if the pattern works with her floorboards or fights them. She can check the colour in her own light, not in studio light. The questions that stopped the sale get answered on the spot.

Second, the sale happens sooner. A shopper who has checked the rug at its true size does not need to sleep on it, ask a friend, or measure the room twice. She knows it fits, so she buys now instead of coming back later and forgetting about it.

Third, the rug stays sold. A buyer who checked the size and the colour before ordering does not send it back for being too big, too small, or the wrong colour in her light. The store keeps the sale and never pays the freight to bring it home again.

Where the view goes on the page

A view that shoppers cannot find does nothing. Put it where the buying decision happens.

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-in-your-room link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same rug in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your shoppers browse.

Start with your bestsellers. Twenty proven rugs covering most of your revenue do more than the whole catalogue on day one. The rugs that bring the most browsers are the rugs that most need the view.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and rug shoppers hesitate most of all, because the whole question is how the rug looks in their room.
