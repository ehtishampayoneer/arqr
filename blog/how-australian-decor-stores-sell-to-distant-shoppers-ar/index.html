How Australian Decor Stores Sell to Distant Shoppers with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a decor store owner in Melbourne. She sells mirrors, lamps, vases, and wall art from one shop and a website. Her pieces ship all over Australia, but most of her online shoppers never visit the shop. They browse from Sydney, Brisbane, Perth, and small regional towns. They like a piece, they like the price, and then they stop. They cannot tell if it will suit their own room, so they close the tab.

The distance is not the problem. The doubt is. A shopper who cannot check the size will not order a mirror from a shop she has never visited. Here is how a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page turns a far-away browser into a buyer.

Why distant shoppers hesitate

A customer in the shop can hold the lamp next to her sofa and see it in seconds. A customer three states away cannot. She has two photos and a list of dimensions. She has to guess whether the mirror is too wide for her mantel, whether the lamp is too tall for her side table, whether the colours will sit right in her light. Most shoppers will not gamble on a guess. They buy from a shop closer to home, or they buy nothing at all.

That is lost revenue a decor store never sees. The shopper never emailed, never complained, never asked a question. She simply left. The store did nothing wrong. The product page just did not answer the one question that mattered.

What changes when the piece appears in their own room

With a true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view on the product page, the distant shopper taps a link on her phone, scans her floor, and the piece appears in her own room at its true size. She walks around it. She checks the mirror against her mantel. She sees if the lamp fits the side table before she pays. The distance stops mattering, because the room in front of her is her own.

That is the whole thing. Your catalogue, placed in the customer's home, through a link. No app to download.

Where the links go

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-at-true-size link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags and the packaging in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same piece in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided, and a QR code in the parcel lets a buyer check the next piece before they order it.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your distant shoppers browse.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and distant shoppers hesitate most of all.
