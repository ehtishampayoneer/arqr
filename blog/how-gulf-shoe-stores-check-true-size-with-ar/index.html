How Gulf Shoe Stores Let Shoppers Check True Size with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a shoe store owner in Dubai. She sells sneakers, boots and leather shoes from one shop and a website that ships across the Gulf. Her returns shelf tells the same story every week. The pairs coming back are not faulty. The notes say the same thing in different handwriting: wrong size. One half size off and the pair travels back at her cost, then sits as an open box pair she has to discount.

The Gulf makes this problem heavier than a single city would. Her shoppers live in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Doha and Kuwait City, and many order from a brand they have never worn. A size 42 in one brand runs longer than a size 42 in another, and boots sit differently from sneakers. Add cross border shipping and a return that would be annoying at home becomes expensive across a border. The pair goes out, comes back, and the margin is gone before it is back on the shelf.

Why shoes come back

Most shoe returns are not about quality. They are about guessing. A shopper reads the size guide, measures their foot once, picks the closest size, and hopes. Photos show the shoe. They do not show it next to the shopper's own foot, at its true size, in the light of their own room. When the box arrives and the length is off by a little, the pair goes back, even when the shopper liked everything else about it.

The cost piles up in three places. The store pays shipping twice. The pair comes back worn on a carpet or not, and staff have to check it, rebox it and discount it. And the shopper, who waited days for delivery, now waits again for the exchange, or buys elsewhere instead. One wrong size can cost a store the sale, the shipping and the customer.

What a true size check changes

With AR (Augmented Reality), the shopper taps a link on the product page and points their phone at their own feet. The shoe appears at its true size, beside or against their own feet, so they can compare it with a pair they already own. That is the honest promise for footwear, and it is a strong one. It ends the length guessing that stops so many orders. It does not tell a shopper how a shoe will feel on their foot, and no honest tool should claim that. What it does, and does well, is show true size before money changes hands.

For a Gulf store, that check matters most at the moment the shopper is furthest away. A customer in Riyadh cannot pop into a Dubai shop to try a pair. A customer in Doha cannot check the length against the shop stock. The AR (Augmented Reality) view brings the check to them, on the phone they are already holding, in the room they are already standing in. No app to download. The view opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code.

One number worth knowing, and it comes from Shopify's own data, not from ARQR360. Shopify reports that products with 3D/AR views convert about 94% better than products without. The reason is plain. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and shoppers ordering across a border hesitate most.

Where the check belongs

First, next to the size selector on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see it at true size link beside the size guide answers the question at the moment it matters, not after the shopper has already closed the tab.

Second, in social media posts and stories. Gulf shoppers browse on phones, and phones run the AR (Augmented Reality) view. A link in a post opens the same pair in the same phone the shopper is already holding.

Third, in the shop itself. A QR code on the price tag lets a visitor take the pair home in their pocket. They check it against their own feet that evening, show it to their family, and order from home. The visit starts the sale. The evening finishes it.
