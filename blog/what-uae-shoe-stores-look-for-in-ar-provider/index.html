What UAE Shoe Stores Should Look for in an AR (Augmented Reality) Provider

Picture a shoe store owner in Dubai. She sells leather shoes and sneakers from one shop and a website that ships across the Emirates and the Gulf. Her product pages get steady traffic. Visitors zoom in on the photos, read the size guide, then close the tab. The shoes sit unsold, and the returns shelf is never empty.

She has heard that AR (Augmented Reality) try-on can help. A shopper points their phone at their own feet and sees the shoe at true size. That sounds right for shoes. Then she starts talking to providers, and every one of them sounds good. So how does she pick?

Ask five plain questions before you sign anything. The answers tell you more than any demo.

1. Can you prove the true size?

For shoes, size is the whole point. A view that is even slightly off teaches the shopper the tool cannot be trusted. Ask the provider how they check each view against the real shoe. Do they build from your real listed dimensions? Do they check the view on a phone against the physical shoe before it goes live?

A provider who cannot describe their size check in one plain paragraph does not have one. Walk away. Your shoppers will compare the view against their own feet, and they will notice.

2. Does the shopper need an app?

This question decides whether anyone actually uses the thing. If your shopper must install an app to try a shoe on, most of them will not. People do not install apps to look at a sneaker.

The version that gets used opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code, in seconds. Ask for a live link you can open on your own phone right now, not a video of someone else using it. Try it yourself on our live footwear demo. It opens in the browser, no install.

3. Who produces the views?

This is the question most stores forget, and it is the one that decides the real cost. A view is not software. Somebody has to produce it for every shoe, in every colour you sell.

Some providers sell you the software and leave the views to you. That means hiring a designer, or paying per view on top of the subscription. Other providers produce the views for you, as part of the service. Photos in, views out, in about seven days.

Ask every provider the same question in writing. Who produces the views, from what do they produce them, and what does new season stock cost? The answers will surprise you.

4. What is the full cost, not just the monthly fee?

Add up everything for one year. The setup fee, the monthly fee, the cost of producing views for your range, and the cost of new stock when the season changes. A small monthly fee with a large per-view charge can cost more than a flat price that includes the views.

5. What happens when your range changes?

A shoe store is not a fixed catalogue. New styles arrive every season. Colours sell out and come back. Ask what happens then. Does each new shoe cost extra? How long does a new view take? What happens to your views if you stop paying?

The right answer for a small store is simple. New stock gets the same treatment as the first batch, at the same price, with no new project each time. Anything else turns every season into a new negotiation.

One honest limit to keep in mind

Be honest about what AR (Augmented Reality) does for shoes and what it does not. It shows the true size and the look. It lets a shopper compare a shoe against their own feet and their own wardrobe. It cannot tell a shopper whether the shoe is comfortable. No view can measure comfort, and any provider who says otherwise is selling you something.

That limit does not weaken the case. Plenty of shoe returns are look returns. The colour is off, the style is bulkier than expected, the shoe does not work with the wardrobe. Those are exactly the returns a true to size view takes away. One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours. Products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without.
