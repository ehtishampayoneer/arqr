ARQR360 vs Matterport: What a Decor Store Actually Gets from Each

Picture a decor store owner in Houston. She sells ceramic vases, wall mirrors, and table lamps online. A friend shows her a Matterport tour of an apartment listing and says, you should do this for your shop. The tour looks stunning. She wonders if it will sell her products.

It might, and it might not. Matterport and ARQR360 solve different problems for a decor store. This is the honest version of which one does what, what each costs, and when each one is worth your money.

What Matterport is

Matterport is the company behind the digital twins you see in real estate listings. It turns a real physical space into a walkable 3D tour on your website. Visitors click through your shop, look around, and zoom in on the shelves. It started in property, and retailers now use it for virtual showroom tours.

The key fact: Matterport captures your space. The customer visits your shop from their couch. Your products never leave your walls.

What ARQR360 is

ARQR360 is a done-for-you AR catalogue for independent decor retailers. You send photos of your bestselling pieces and your real listed dimensions. We build true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) views from them and hand you links and QR codes that open each view in the customer's phone browser. No app, no platform to learn, no 3D team.

The key fact: ARQR360 puts each product in the customer's room. The customer checks whether your mirror fits their wall, not whether your shop looks nice.

Pricing: per-space subscription vs one flat rate

Matterport publishes its plans. Current listings put the Starter plan around $10 a month for a few Active Spaces, and Pro and Business from $69 a month, with Enterprise on quote. The subscription covers the hosting of your captured spaces. The capture itself is extra: you scan with a phone or a 360 camera, or you pay for their Capture Services in hundreds of cities. Their pro LiDAR camera costs thousands of dollars. So the real first-year cost is the subscription plus whatever you spend getting the space captured well.

ARQR360 publishes its price: $199 one-time setup and $19 a month, locked for life for the first 50 stores. Every AR (Augmented Reality) view is built and hosted as part of it. No product cap, no per-view fee, no capture hardware to buy. You already have the photos.

Setup effort: scanning your space vs sending photos

A Matterport project means capturing your showroom. You clear the space, scan every room, process the model, and keep it updated as your displays change. A rescan is a real job, so shops tend to do it once and watch it go stale as stock rotates. That is fine for a flagship that rarely changes. It is work for a store that refreshes displays every season.

With ARQR360 the project is: send photos of your bestselling vases, mirrors, and lamps, bestsellers first. About seven days later your AR views are live, as links on your product pages and QR codes you can put anywhere. New pieces get built the same way when stock changes. Nothing to scan, nothing to learn.

Who builds the 3D content?

With Matterport, the 3D twin comes from scans you make or pay for. The quality of the tour depends on the capture: phone scans work, a pro camera or a paid capture service looks better, and each one costs money or time. The views of your individual products are not part of it. A Matterport twin shows your shop, not a clickable version of each vase.

With ARQR360, the view building is the service. We build the AR (Augmented Reality) views from your product photos and your real listed dimensions. You never touch modelling software and you never hire a 3D team. Your first view is free, so you can check the quality on your own phone before spending anything.

True-to-size accuracy

Matterport's twins are dimensionally accurate. Published comparisons put their measurements within about one percent of reality, which is why agents and architects trust them. Your customers can walk your shop and trust the distances they see.

ARQR360 views are built to your exact listed dimensions, so a 24-inch mirror appears 24 inches wide on the customer's wall. On accuracy both aim for the same thing. The difference is what the customer measures: your space in one case, your product in theirs.

They answer different questions

This is the part that matters. A Matterport tour answers: what does this shop look like, and what is on the shelves. That is a browsing question. It helps a customer who is deciding whether to visit or who wants the showroom experience online.

An ARQR360 view answers: will this exact piece fit my room. That is a buying question. Decor returns are almost always size returns. The vase looked right online and overwhelmed the side table. The mirror got swallowed by the wall. When the customer checks the real size before buying, those returns fall. For the returns side, read how home decor stores cut returns with AR.

Shopify reports that products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. That is Shopify's own data, not ours. The mechanism is simple: shoppers who can see the true size hesitate less.

When Matterport is the better pick

Be honest about your store. Matterport fits better if your showroom itself sells: a beautiful space customers would visit if they could, a flagship you want visible worldwide, a trade buyer who wants to walk the floor before a big order. If the space is the product, scan the space.

FAQ

Does Matterport put my products in the customer's room?

No. Matterport captures your space, so customers walk through your showroom online. It does not create individual AR views of your products at true size in the customer's own room. Those are two different jobs.

Do I need special hardware for Matterport?

You can scan with a phone or a 360 camera. The best-looking captures use their pro LiDAR camera, which costs thousands of dollars, or their paid capture services. Budget the capture on top of the subscription.
