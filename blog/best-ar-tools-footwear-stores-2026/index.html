The Best AR Tools for Footwear Stores in 2026

Picture a shoe store owner in Toronto. She sells leather boots online and from one small shop. Her product pages get steady traffic. Visitors zoom in on the photos, read the size guide, then close the tab. The boots sit unsold.

Footwear is the hardest category to sell sight unseen. Fit and look are personal. AR try-on answers both at once: the customer points their phone at their feet and sees the shoe on their own foot, at true size, in the light of their own room. Here are the real options a footwear store can buy in 2026, written for a store owner, not a developer.

Option 1: WANNA (Perfect Corp)

WANNA is the company behind the Wanna Kicks sneaker try-on, now part of Perfect Corp., the beauty and fashion tech company behind the widely used YouCam apps. Perfect acquired Wannaby from Farfetch in late 2024, and the platform now covers sneakers, heels, boots, and sandals, running across web and mobile apps. Its try-on technology has served more than 30 luxury brands.

Good when: you are a brand or a large retailer with a digital team and a budget sized for enterprise software. You get serious foot-tracking tech with multiple viewing angles. Bad when: you are a small store. This is sold as a platform to brands, not a plug-and-play product for a 40-style catalogue. Pricing is quote-based, so get a written quote for your own range before you compare.

Option 2: Shopify AR viewer apps

If you sell on Shopify, AR viewer apps on the app store add a 3D viewer to your product pages for a small monthly subscription. Public listings put pricing around free to roughly $36 a month, depending on how many products you publish.

The catch is the same one every viewer app has. The apps are viewers only. You supply every 3D model yourself, usually commissioned from 3D artists at roughly $100 to $500 per product. A shoe store with 40 styles in three colours needs around 120 models for full coverage. The subscription is small. The modelling bill is the project. We worked the same maths in our pricing breakdown for footwear stores.

Good when: you already have 3D models of your shoes, or a designer on staff who makes them. Bad when: you do not, which is almost every small shoe store.

Option 3: 3D and AR creation services

Some companies build the 3D models and host the AR for you. Seek is the well-known example in footwear. In a podcast interview, their CEO described the pricing plainly: you pay per model for creation, roughly a couple hundred dollars a model depending on complexity, plus a SaaS fee for hosting and distribution that scales with your catalogue size. We compared that route in detail in ARQR360 vs Seek for footwear stores.

Good when: you run a brand with hundreds of styles and the budget for per-model creation plus platform fees. Bad when: you run a small store, where the creation bill is the whole project before anything earns a dollar.

Option 4: done-for-you AR services

You send photos and real dimensions of your shoes, bestsellers first. Someone else builds the true-to-size 3D models and hands you links and QR codes that open try-on in the phone browser. ARQR360 works this way. No app for your customers. No modelling work on your side. About seven days from photos to a live catalogue.

The price is flat and public: $199 one-time setup and $19 a month, locked for life for the first 50 stores, with all model building included. No product cap, no per-view fees.

Good when: you want AR live this month with no new hires. Bad when: you need deep custom work like a full product configurator. That is a platform job, not a service job.

What to check before you choose

  • True-to-size proof: for shoes, a model even slightly off teaches the customer the tool cannot be trusted. Ask every provider how they verify scale against the real shoe.
  • The app question: if your customer must install an app, most of them will not. Browser try-on gets used. App try-on gets skipped.
  • Who makes the models: the biggest cost on most routes is model creation, not the software. Ask whether models are included and what new-season stock costs.
  • Total cost, not the monthly fee: add the models, the setup, and the new range. Per-model fees quietly dwarf subscriptions on catalogues that change.

Where each option lands

If you are a brand with a digital team and an enterprise budget, WANNA's try-on tech is a serious option with the foot-tracking to show for it. If you already have 3D models and a Shopify store, a viewer app is a cheap add. If you have neither, a done-for-you service is the route that ends with AR actually live on your site.

One more number, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Whichever route you take, giving shoppers a true-to-size try-on beats a flat photo.
