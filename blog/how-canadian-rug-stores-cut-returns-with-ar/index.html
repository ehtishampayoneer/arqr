How Canadian Rug Stores Cut Returns with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Toronto. She sells wool and handmade rugs from one shop and a website. Her bestsellers ship across Ontario, and a good share go further, to Vancouver, Calgary, and Montreal. The rugs sell well online. The problem starts after the sale. A rug arrives, the customer unrolls it, and it is the wrong size for the room. Back it goes.

Size returns are the quiet cost of selling rugs online in Canada. The country is big, the shipping is long, and a returned rug costs the store twice: the freight back, and a rug that has been unrolled and rolled again. Most of those returns were never about quality. The rug was fine. It was the wrong size, and the customer only found out when it was on their floor.

Why rug size is so hard to guess from a photo

A flat photo hides the one thing that matters. A 5 by 8 looks generous on a product page and small under a real dining table. A 9 by 12 looks huge in a thumbnail and just right in an open plan living room. The customer has no reference point except the size guide, and few shoppers measure their room before they order.

Picture a shopper in Ottawa. She likes a cream wool rug for the living room. She measures the wall, not the floor space the rug will cover. She orders the 8 by 10. It arrives, and the front legs of the sofa sit off the rug. It looks wrong, and back it goes. Nothing was wrong with the rug. The guess was wrong.

What changes when the shopper sees it at true size

AR (Augmented Reality) lets the shopper point their phone at their own floor and see the rug there at its true size. They walk around it. They check it against the sofa, the table, and the doorway. The question that causes the return gets answered before the order is placed, not after the rug arrives.

That is the whole mechanism. It is not a fancy effect. It is the rug, at its true size, on the shopper's own floor, in their own light. If the view gets the size right, the shopper trusts it. If the view is even a little off, the shopper learns the tool cannot be trusted, and the sale is gone anyway.

Three checks that stop most rug returns

First, the size check. The shopper sees the rug under their own table before they buy. An 8 by 10 that swallows the room shows itself at once, and so does a 5 by 8 that leaves bare floor around the edges.

Second, the pattern check. A bold pattern looks different at full size on a real floor than it does in a cropped photo. The shopper sees the whole rug, not a corner of it, and decides with the full picture in front of them.

Third, the room check. The rug has to live with the sofa, the coffee table, and the light in that room. The shopper sees it there, in their own room, before the order. Three checks, all before the sale, and each one removes a reason to send the rug back.
