How New Zealand Shoe Stores Cut Size Returns with AR (True Size, No App)

Picture a shoe store owner in Auckland. She stocks running shoes, school shoes, winter boots. Her online orders grow every month, and so does the stack of courier bags coming back. Almost none of the returns are faulty. The labels all say the same thing: wrong size. One half size off and the pair comes back. She pays the freight both ways and puts a worn-once pair back on the shelf as an open box she has to discount.

This is the plain-English version of why that happens and what actually fixes it.

Why shoes come back

Most shoe returns are not quality problems. They are size problems. A size 8 in one brand runs longer than a size 8 in another. Boots sit differently from sneakers. Photos cannot show any of that. The customer reads the size chart, skims the reviews, and guesses. A "true to size" comment from someone with a narrow foot does not help a shopper with a wide one. When the guess is wrong, the pair comes back. In New Zealand the cost of that guess bites harder than in big markets. Freight between the islands and out to rural addresses adds up, and a returned pair can sit in transit for a week before it is even back on your shelf.

What AR can and cannot do for a shoe store

Honesty first, because it matters here. No screen can tell a shopper how a shoe will feel on their foot. Cushion, grip, the way a leather boot breaks in, none of that shows on a phone. Any provider that promises fit is selling something AR cannot deliver.

What AR (Augmented Reality) can do is show true size. A shopper points their phone at their own feet and the shoe appears next to them at its real size. They get a real size idea: how long the pair runs, how wide it looks next to their own foot, how the proportions sit. That is the question that drives most size returns. Is this a big 9 or a small 9. AR answers it with their own eyes instead of a size chart. The shoppers who order unsure are the ones who send shoes back. Remove the doubt and a good share of the returns never leave the store.

Why the phone browser matters

Here is the part most shoe stores get wrong. AR that lives inside an app dies quietly. Shoppers will not install an app to check one pair of boots. A shopper who is on the fence taps once and sees the shoe at true size within seconds. That is the only version of AR that survives contact with a real customer.

The bottom line for a Kiwi shoe store

You cannot stop every size return. Some shoppers sit between sizes and will always gamble. But most size returns come from the ones who ordered unsure. A true-size AR view turns an unsure order into a sure one, or into no order at all. Either way the pair does not travel to Invercargill and back on your money.
