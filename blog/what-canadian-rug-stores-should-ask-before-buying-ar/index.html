What Canadian Rug Stores Should Ask Before Buying AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Toronto. She has sold rugs for ten years. Her shoppers ask the same question every week. Will this rug fit my room. Will the colour suit my sofa. She knows AR (Augmented Reality) can answer both questions before the order. So she starts shopping for an AR provider. Every website promises the same thing. Fast. Easy. True to size. The prices range from cheap to shocking. She has no way to tell which promise is real.

This article is the shortlist she needs. Five questions. Ask them of every provider. A good provider answers each one in plain words. A weak provider hides behind jargon or changes the subject.

1. Does the shopper need an app

Ask this first. If the shopper has to install an app, most shoppers will not. They are standing in their living room with a phone in one hand. They will not stop to download, sign up, and wait. They will close the tab and buy from the next store.

A good answer sounds like this. No app. Each rug gets a link and a QR code. The shopper scans it and the rug appears in the phone browser. That is the whole step. Ask the provider to show you on your own phone, not on a video. If they cannot show it live, the answer is no.

2. Is the rug shown at its true size

True to size is the whole point. A rug that looks right and measures wrong still comes back. Ask how the size is set. The provider should build the view from your listed dimensions, the real length and width of the rug you sell. Ask what happens with a round rug, or a runner, or a rug with an uneven edge. A provider that guesses the size from a photo alone is guessing.

Check it yourself. Ask for a test view of one rug you already stock. Put your phone next to the real rug in your own shop. The AR view should match the real rug, edge to edge. If it does not match, nothing else the provider says matters.

Most rug stores have no design team and no wish to build one. Ask who turns your product photos into AR views. If the answer is you, walk away. Your job is selling rugs, not learning design software.

4. What does it cost, all of it

Ask for the full price, not the headline price. Some providers charge per view, per product, or per month per rug. A store with 200 rugs can watch a small monthly fee turn into a big one. Ask about setup fees, per-product fees, and what happens when you add a new rug next month.

Ask who answers when a view stops working or a new rug needs adding. A provider that disappears after the sale leaves you with a dead catalogue. Ask for a named contact and a real response time. Ask how new rugs get added and how fast.
