How European Rug Stores Win Christmas Sales with AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a rug store owner in Lyon. She sells wool rugs, runners and flatweaves from one shop and a website. November arrives and the Christmas shoppers arrive with it. They browse her product pages in the evening, find a rug they like, and stop. They cannot tell if it will suit the room it is meant for. The room is not theirs. It belongs to the person getting the gift, or it is their own living room and they still cannot picture the size.

That shopper is not saying no. She is saying she cannot tell yet. At Christmas the doubt is doubled. A rug is a big gift and a big piece. It has to fit the room, sit right under the table, and look right with the furniture already there. Some shoppers buy anyway and guess wrong. Those rugs come back in January. The rest never buy at all. Both cost the store, and both are avoidable.

Why Christmas rugs are hard to buy from a photo

A photo of a rug on a white background gives the shopper nothing to measure against. No sofa, no table, no doorway. A 160 by 230 rug and a 200 by 290 rug can look the same in a square photo. Pattern scale is worse. A pattern that looks bold on screen can look busy across a whole living room floor, or fade away entirely.

Gift buying makes it worse. The giver cannot walk into the recipient's home with a tape measure. She is guessing twice: the taste and the size. When the size guess fails, the return lands in January, right when the store is quietest. A return in January costs freight both ways and a sale that looked safe in December.

What the shopper actually checks

AR (Augmented Reality) answers one question. How does this rug look at its true size, on a real floor? The shopper points their phone at their own living room floor. The rug appears at its true size, so they can check it against the sofa, the coffee table and the doorway. Too big is obvious. Too small is obvious. The guessing ends.

That check works best on the pieces that already get traffic. Bestsellers first. Twenty proven rugs covering most of your Christmas revenue teach you what shoppers actually do with the views, before you cover the whole catalogue.

Why the browser view gets used

Ask one question before you buy any AR route: does the shopper need to download an app? If the answer is yes, most of them will not use it. People do not install apps to check a rug. A view that opens in the phone browser, from a link or a QR code, gets used. Test that on your own phone with our live rug demo.

One number worth knowing, and it is Shopify's own data, not ours: products with 3D or AR views convert about 94% better than products without. Shoppers who can check the true size hesitate less, and at Christmas hesitation is a lost sale.

Where the links go

  • Next to the price on every product page. That is where size doubt lives. A see-it-on-your-floor link beside the buy button answers the question at the moment it matters.
  • On the price tags in the shop. A QR code on the tag opens the same rug in AR. Customers check it at home and come back decided, or send the link to the person they are buying for.
  • In Instagram posts and stories. The link opens on a phone, which is exactly where your Christmas shoppers browse.
