Why Table Lamps Come Back, and How UK Lighting Stores Stop the Guessing with AR

Picture a lighting store owner in Leeds. She sells table lamps, pendants, and floor lamps from one shop and a website that ships across the UK. A customer in Bristol orders her bestselling ceramic table lamp from the product page. It arrives, she puts it on her wide sideboard, and it looks lost. A toy lamp on a big piece of furniture. Back it comes. The store pays the courier both ways and relists a lamp that was never faulty. Nothing was wrong with the lamp. The scale was wrong.

This is the honest version of most lighting returns. They are not quality problems. They are scale problems.

Why lighting is a scale product

On a product page, every lamp looks fine. A 40cm table lamp and a 55cm one look like the same lamp at different prices. Numbers on a dimensions tab do not translate. Shoppers measure the sideboard, imagine the lamp, and guess. The guess is wrong often enough to cost real money.

Lighting is worse than most decor for this, because a lamp is never judged on its own. It is judged against the furniture under it and the space around it. A table lamp has to look right against the sideboard or the bedside table. A pendant hangs at a height that has to clear heads and clear the table. A floor lamp has to stand in a room without crowding the walkway. Interior designers check these relationships by habit. Regular buyers do not carry those habits in their heads. They buy the lamp they love and find out at home that love was not enough.

What the AR view does

A true-to-size AR (Augmented Reality) view ends the guessing. The buyer taps a link on the product page, points the phone at her own sideboard, and the lamp appears at its real listed size. She steps back. She checks it against the furniture it has to live with. She sees the wrong size before money changes hands.

No app. It opens in the phone browser, from the product page or a QR code. The buyer installs nothing and needs no instructions.

The pendant problem deserves its own paragraph

Pendants are the hardest lighting sale online. Drop height is everything, and drop height is a number nobody can picture. Hang it too low and it blocks the view across the table. Hang it too high and it looks like a ceiling decoration with no purpose. Shoppers read the cable length, guess at the ceiling height, and order the wrong thing or order nothing.

When the buyer can point her phone at the dining table and see the pendant hanging at its real size, the guessing stops. She moves the phone up and down, checks the clearance, and picks the right drop or the right size shade. The store sells more pendants because the buyer can buy with confidence, and fewer of them come back.
