How UK Furniture Stores Answer the Will-It-Fit-Up-the-Stairs Question With True-Size AR (Augmented Reality)

Picture a furniture store owner in Manchester. She sells sofas and armchairs from a showroom and a website. She knows her fabric, her prices, and her delivery routes. And every week she hears the same question, in the shop and in the chat box: "Lovely sofa. But will it fit up my stairs?"

That question kills more sales than the price does.

Why "will it fit" beats price as a deal breaker

The customer is not worried about the money. They are picturing their hallway. A Victorian terrace with a staircase that turns halfway up. A front door that opens onto a narrow hall. A flat above a shop with a stairwell built for people, not sofas.

A tape measure helps, but it leaves too much to the imagination. The customer measures the door, checks the dimensions on your website, and still cannot picture the turn at the top of the stairs. So they do the safe thing. They close the tab, or they say "I will think about it" and never come back.

Your store pays for that doubt twice. Once in the lost sale, and again when a sofa does get ordered on hope and comes back. Failed deliveries and returns on large pieces are the most expensive returns in furniture retail.

The three spots every UK shopper checks

Talk to any delivery team and they will name the same three trouble spots.

The front door. Standard UK doors are narrow, and the frame is narrower than the number on the tape. The sofa has to clear it at an angle, not head on.

The stair turn. This is where most failures happen. The sofa climbs fine, then meets the half landing turn and will not swing around. No photo on a product page shows this.

The room itself. Even when the sofa gets in, the customer worried it would swallow the room. They want to see it in place, against their own wall, before they commit.

Three checks, all about the customer's own home. That is why nothing on your website, however good the photos, can answer them. The answer has to happen in their hallway.

What it does for your delivery team

Here is the part store owners feel in the accounts. When the customer has checked the fit themselves, the delivery stops being a gamble. Some stores now ask the customer to run the AR (Augmented Reality) check before the delivery is booked. Fewer failed drops, fewer "it would not go up the stairs" returns, fewer two man crews sent out for nothing.
